8 Wolverine Comic Storylines That Would Make Incredible Video Games

By Peter D’Souza
WOLVERINE
Fourth Frame
List / 02

Wolverine has decades of comics to draw from, and a game need not always be built around how many enemies Logan can cut down. Some of his best stories ask what happens when the claws are useless, unwanted or beyond his control. Here are eight games I would like to play.

8. Wolverine: Origin (Jenkins/Kubert)

Start with James Howlett before he knows what he is. Most of the game would be spent moving through his family home, talking and making small choices. A hidden rage meter occasionally takes control away; the screen goes black, then returns to the aftermath. You would dread the moments you cannot remember.

7. Kitty Pryde and Wolverine (Claremont)

Play as teenage Kitty, slipping through walls and floors to solve problems Logan cannot punch. Wolverine follows as an AI companion and steps in when things turn violent. Their lessons could unlock new ways to use Kitty's phasing, making the mentor relationship something the player practises rather than watches.

6. Uncanny X-Force (Remender)

Give Logan a team of Deadpool, Psylocke, Fantomex and Archangel, then send them on missions the X-Men will not approve. Imagine squad tactics where a successful objective can still cost a teammate's trust. The question after each mission is whether the team will keep following Wolverine when his methods get harder to defend.

5. Death of Wolverine (Soule)

Take away the healing factor. A broken arm in one mission should still hurt in the next unless you spend time and supplies treating it. Put the adamantium poisoning on a clock. Now every fight risks shortening the time Logan has left, and avoiding a battle can be the smart move.

4. Enemy of the State (Millar)

Open with a playable stretch of Wolverine under enemy control, cutting through heroes he would normally protect. Later, make him fight to regain agency. The second act should have to live with damage the player caused in the first, instead of letting a change of allegiance wipe it away.

3. Old Man Logan (Millar/McNiven)

Make Logan cross a ruined America while refusing to use his claws. Travel, supplies and the people he meets would matter more than winning fights. When he finally breaks that rule, it should be because the journey has made the decision painful, not because the tutorial has ended.

2. Weapon X (Barry Windsor-Smith)

Begin inside the Weapon X facility and keep the player uncertain about what Logan can survive. A stealth escape would suit Barry Windsor-Smith's story better than a sequence of boss fights. Each injury could leave him less sure of what the experiment has done to his body.

1. Wolverine (1982 miniseries, Claremont/Miller)

Keep the game in Japan and build its fights around timing, restraint and duels. Mariko Yashida should matter to the choices Logan makes between battles. A focused adventure of fifteen or twenty hours could give this story more room than another map crowded with distractions.
