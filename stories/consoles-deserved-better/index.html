4 Consoles That Deserved Way Better Than They Got

By Peter D’Souza
LOST SYSTEMS
Fourth Frame
List / 06

Sales figures decide which machines stay on shelves. They do a poor job of explaining why people loved playing them. These four systems gave us hardware ideas and game libraries worth revisiting, even though none won its market.

1. Sega Dreamcast

The Dreamcast launched in Japan in November 1998 with a modem built in. Phantasy Star Online later made that connection feel essential by letting friends explore together. Sega's arcade hardware also had much in common with the console, and games such as Crazy Taxi and Marvel vs. Capcom 2 came home with their energy intact. Sega put enormous resources into games such as Shenmue, but the Dreamcast sold about 9.13 million units before the company ended production in 2001. Its short life left a library with a distinct arcade flavour.

2. Neo Geo Pocket Color

The Neo Geo Pocket Color had an excellent clicky joystick and a library built around SNK's fighting and arcade games. King of Fighters R-2, Metal Slug and Card Fighters' Clash made a strong case for the little handheld. It could even connect to a Dreamcast in supported games, while Sega published Sonic the Hedgehog: Pocket Adventure for it. That combination still feels unlikely.

3. WonderSwan

Gunpei Yokoi designed the WonderSwan after leaving Nintendo, but died before its 1999 launch. The machine could be held horizontally or vertically, and its monochrome model ran for an unusually long time on one AA battery. Square supported it with Final Fantasy ports and other releases. It stayed in Japan, which meant much of the world never had the chance to see what Yokoi built after the Game Boy.

4. PlayStation Vita

The Vita's OLED screen and dual sticks made a strong first impression. Gravity Rush, Persona 4 Golden and Uncharted: Golden Abyss gave players reasons to keep it charged. Its proprietary memory cards added a frustrating cost, and Sony's first-party support faded. Production ended in 2019. The handheld still has a devoted following, which says a lot about what it got right.
