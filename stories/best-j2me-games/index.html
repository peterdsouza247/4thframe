5 J2ME Games That Proved Mobile Phones Could Handle Real Games

By Peter D’Souza
J2ME Games
Fourth Frame
List / 03

Long before smartphones, Gameloft treated Java phones as a place to make proper games. The hardware was awkward: small screens, wildly different handsets and a keypad where a controller should be. The good releases found a way through those limits.

1. Prince of Persia: Warrior Within

The 2004 phone version turns Warrior Within into a 2D platformer while keeping the sword fighting, stolen weapons and time tricks. It plays like a game designed for a keypad, rather than a tiny copy of the PS2 release.

2. Splinter Cell: Chaos Theory

Stealth is difficult to communicate on a screen this small. Chaos Theory still asks you to watch patrols, choose your route and avoid being seen. Making that legible across different phones took considerable work from Gameloft's developers.

3. Roland-Garros 2009

Gameloft brought the French Open to J2ME handsets in a dedicated tennis game. Serving, moving into position and timing a return all had to work on physical phone keys. Sports games belonged on these devices too, even if the controls needed rethinking.

4. Real Football

Gameloft kept Real Football going through annual releases in the Java era. For players without a console close by, a football match on the phone in your pocket was a convincing pitch on its own.

5. Asphalt

Asphalt began on keypad phones and followed players onto smartphones and consoles. Few series from that mobile era made the same transition. Its early entries are worth revisiting for the racing they managed to fit onto those screens.
