7 Beloved NES Games That Deserve More Credit

By Peter D’Souza
NES GEMS
Fourth Frame
List / 05

Ask anyone for the best NES games and you'll get the same six answers every time — Mario, Zelda, Mega Man, Castlevania. Fair enough, they earned it. But there's a whole second tier of NES games that fans quietly adore, critics respected at the time, and modern "best of" lists keep skipping past. These are the ones worth another look.

1. Duck Hunt

Bundled with nearly every NES sold in North America, Duck Hunt's laughing dog became one of gaming's most enduring characters purely through spite — an entire generation grew up wanting to reach through the screen and shoot him. Less well known: in Game A, a second player can take control of the duck with a controller while the first player aims the Zapper. That small twist turns a familiar solo game into an unexpectedly mischievous contest.

2. Sky Destroyer

Released in Japan during the NES's oddly quiet first year — when the console was still finding its audience — Sky Destroyer quietly filled the gap with a surprisingly smooth WWII dogfighting game, full 360-degree movement and scrolling included. It rarely makes "best of" lists today, but it was doing genuinely technically impressive work while most of the library was still finding its feet.

3. Chip 'n Dale: Rescue Rangers

Some critics rank this co-op Capcom platformer alongside Super Mario Bros. 3 and Castlevania III as one of the best the console ever produced — a claim that sounds absurd until you actually play it. The box-throwing combat, tight two-player co-op, and Yasuhiro Kawasaki's soundtrack hold up remarkably well decades later, and it remains one of the most requested licensed games fans want to see officially re-released.

4. Blaster Master

Sunsoft's genre-mixing oddity — side-scrolling tank exploration that opens into top-down action when you step outside your vehicle — was doing Metroidvania-style interconnected map design years before that was a recognised genre. It's the kind of game that shows up on "hidden gem" lists constantly and mainstream "best of" lists almost never.

5. StarTropics

Nintendo's own weird, underrated action-adventure is best remembered today for one legendarily bizarre puzzle: a mid-game letter instructed players to physically dip the game's included paper manual in water to reveal a hidden code, without which the game was literally unsolvable. It's one of the strangest and most genuinely inventive design decisions in NES history.

6. Little Nemo: The Dream Master

Capcom's gorgeously animated platformer, based on an obscure early-1900s comic strip revived briefly as an 80s anime, is routinely praised for its art direction and creature-riding mechanics — you eat candy to tame and ride enemies as mounts — and just as routinely forgotten because almost nobody outside Japan had heard of the source material.

7. Crystalis

Released late in the NES's life by SNK, this post-apocalyptic action-RPG is frequently cited by retro enthusiasts as one of the most ambitious, forward-thinking games the console ever got — real-time combat, elemental swords, a genuinely large overworld — and it's routinely overlooked simply because it arrived after most people had already moved on to the SNES.
