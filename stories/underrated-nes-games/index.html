7 Beloved NES Games That Deserve More Credit

By Peter D’Souza
NES GEMS
Fourth Frame
List / 05

Mario and Zelda dominate most conversations about the NES. They should. I still want to make room for the games I remember for stranger reasons: an insufferable dog, a tank you can climb out of, and a puzzle that needed water rather than button presses.

1. Duck Hunt

That laughing dog made missing a shot feel personal. In Game A, another player can control the duck with a second controller while you aim the Zapper. It is a small feature, but it can turn a familiar round into a contest between two people on the couch.

2. Sky Destroyer

Sky Destroyer puts you behind a plane and lets you weave around enemy fire while ships appear below. Its movement and scrolling make the fights feel unusually lively for an early Famicom game. I remember the freedom of steering through those battles more than the game's place in any rankings.

3. Chip 'n Dale: Rescue Rangers

Capcom built a co-op platformer around picking up crates, throwing them and getting out of your partner's way. The levels move quickly, and the music sticks. Playing with someone else is the best way to see why Rescue Rangers has lasted in people's memories.

4. Blaster Master

Blaster Master starts with a vehicle built for side-scrolling exploration, then lets Jason get out and enter top-down areas on foot. Going back to the tank changes your sense of what you can reach. Sunsoft made those changes of scale feel like part of one continuous journey.

5. StarTropics

At one point StarTropics asks you to dip a letter supplied with the game in water to reveal a code. It is a wonderful trick if you still have the letter, and a problem if you don't. The puzzle ties the physical box to the adventure in a way a menu hint never could.

6. Little Nemo: The Dream Master

The candy in Little Nemo is useful for more than scoring points. Feed it to certain creatures and Nemo can ride them, borrowing different abilities to cross a level. Capcom's animation makes the dream world inviting even when the platforming gets tough.

7. Crystalis

Crystalis gives you a large world to cross and swords with different elemental powers. Combat happens in real time, and finding the right weapon changes which obstacles you can get past. It is an ambitious action RPG that still rewards wandering off the obvious route.
