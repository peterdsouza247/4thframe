5 Cancelled Games That Robbed Us Of Potential Masterpieces

By Peter D’Souza
WHAT IF?
Fourth Frame
List / 07

A cancelled game leaves room for the version we invent in our heads. These five showed enough to make that version tempting: a descent into Coruscant, a multiplayer Albion, a new Prince of Persia, a terrifying hallway and a dragon on Xbox. None was guaranteed to succeed, which makes their unfinished ideas more interesting to revisit.

1. Star Wars 1313

Set in Coruscant's criminal underworld, 1313 traded lightsabers for a bounty-hunter story built around gadgets and gunplay. A young Boba Fett was reportedly considered for the lead. The 2012 demonstration made the underworld itself the attraction, taking Star Wars down into its dangerous lower levels. After Disney acquired Lucasfilm, LucasArts stopped internal development in April 2013 and shifted toward licensing games to outside studios. Work on 1313 stopped with it. The claim that Disney thought it too mature has circulated for years, but the announced licensing shift is the reason we can document.

2. Fable Legends

Lionhead imagined Fable as a four-versus-one adventure. Four heroes would cooperate while a fifth player controlled the villain, with a free-to-play model layered on top. It was a sharp turn for a series built on individual choices. In March 2016, Microsoft ended development and began consulting employees about Lionhead's proposed closure. The studio ultimately shut down. Microsoft's announcement did not cite fan reaction or the beta as the cause, leaving the design experiment unfinished and its failure harder to explain than a single headline allows.

3. Prince of Persia: Redemption

A pre-rendered pitch for a cinematic Prince of Persia game sat on YouTube for years before fans rediscovered it in 2020. The video showed time manipulation, a collapsing city and spectral enemies. Former Ubisoft animator Jonathan Cooper confirmed it was target footage, not a playable slice, and said its convincing imitation of gameplay inspired the team's pitch for Assassin's Creed III. Redemption never became the game in that video, but its presentation still reached another project.

4. Silent Hills

Kojima, Guillermo del Toro and Norman Reedus working on a new Silent Hill was an arresting prospect. P.T. made it immediate: its looping hallway frightened players without showing them the game it advertised. In April 2015 Konami confirmed Silent Hills would not continue, and the teaser left the PlayStation Store. Konami did not give a detailed public account of the decision. What we can still play, if we kept a copy, is the strangest surviving piece of the project.

5. Scalebound

PlatinumGames showed an Xbox action RPG about Drew fighting alongside a dragon called Thuban. The demonstrations promised large battles and cooperative play. Microsoft cancelled Scalebound in January 2017. Platinum later acknowledged difficulty meeting the project's technical ambitions, including Unreal Engine and online features. It is easy to imagine the finished dragon game; it is harder to know whether the pieces shown publicly could ever have fit together.

Of the five, I most want to know what Silent Hills would have done after that hallway. But perhaps the loss is different for everyone. A cancelled game never has to contend with its own ending, and that makes it easy to remember the possibilities and forget the problems.
