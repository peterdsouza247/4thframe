Games / The Elder Scrolls

Choose a province. Make your own beginning.

Each main Elder Scrolls game introduces a new hero, a different province, and a local crisis. Shared history rewards curiosity, but the numbered games are designed to work as individual adventures.

Our starting recommendation: Begin with Skyrim for an accessible open-world introduction. Choose Oblivion Remastered for a more traditional fantasy quest, or Morrowind if discovery and an unfamiliar culture matter more than modern conveniences.

Choose your way in.

I want an accessible world to explore

Skyrim

Its opening establishes the immediate crisis, while guilds and side quests offer ways to find your own interests. You can understand the adventure without any earlier game.

Know this much
Dragons have returned to a province divided by civil war.
You can skip
Arena through Oblivion, Online, lore videos, and completing every guild.
The trade-off
Its combat and conversation systems are simpler than some earlier entries. Avoid treating the enormous quest list as homework.
Go next
Follow the main quest or a faction that interests you. Dawnguard and Dragonborn are optional story expansions.

I want a traditional fantasy quest

Oblivion / Oblivion Remastered

A clear crisis and a central ally provide a strong narrative spine, with towns and guilds around it. The remaster tells the same story as the original.

Know this much
The Empire faces a succession crisis and dangerous gates opening across Cyrodiil.
You can skip
The first three numbered games and Skyrim.
The trade-off
The original game’s systems and the remaster’s presentation differ, but neither requires prior lore. Level scaling can affect the feel of progression.
Go next
Try the guilds you like, then Shivering Isles for a distinctive separate realm. Knights of the Nine is another optional quest line.

I want unusual culture and patient discovery

Morrowind

Its local religions, political houses, and unfamiliar landscape make learning the world part of the adventure.

Know this much
You arrive in Vvardenfell amid competing accounts of prophecy and authority.
You can skip
Arena and Daggerfall, a complete map checklist, and all the later games.
The trade-off
Directions, chance-based attacks, and older interfaces demand patience. Reading and asking questions matter more than following markers.
Go next
Finish the main story if it holds you, then choose Tribunal or Bloodmoon. Neither is preparation for Oblivion.

I want shared adventures and many provinces

The Elder Scrolls Online

A separate historical era and its own introductions make Online an independent entrance into Tamriel.

Know this much
The game is set long before Skyrim, amid rival alliances and supernatural crises.
You can skip
The numbered games and every previous Online chapter.
The trade-off
MMO progression, optional paid content, and the order of accessible quest lines can make the catalogue feel larger than the story you actually need.
Go next
Choose one coherent campaign or zone story. Use its quest guide before adding more chapters.

Enough to begin.

Tamriel

A continent of provinces with competing cultures, rulers, and accounts of their past.

A new hero each time

Arena, Daggerfall, Morrowind, Oblivion, and Skyrim do not follow one returning player character. There is no save-import chain to complete.

History is disputed

Religions, books, and witnesses often disagree. Treat an in-world account as someone’s perspective rather than an unquestionable encyclopedia entry.

The bigger picture.

The Empire and provinces

Imperial authority changes across eras. Local cultures and independence movements have their own interests.

Aedra and Daedra

These divine groupings help explain different religions. Daedric princes are individual powers with distinct spheres, not one team.

Guilds and local houses

Mage, warrior, thief, religious, and political organisations offer different paths. Joining all of them is optional.

Online’s alliances

The three alliances belong to Online’s historical conflict. They are not prerequisites for understanding Skyrim’s factions.

Sources and scope

Reviewed 3 October 2026. Entry points, optional routes, and play order are Fourth Frame’s editorial recommendations.

Covers Arena, Daggerfall, Morrowind and its two expansions, Oblivion and selected story expansions, Skyrim and its two expansions, plus an orientation to Online’s original main quest. Oblivion Remastered and Skyrim editions retell existing campaigns. Online’s chapters, DLC, and seasons are a separate optional catalogue; its 2025 and 2026 updates should not be mistaken for numbered sequels. Battlespire, Redguard, mobile games, and every Online zone are outside this introductory route. The Elder Scrolls VI is listed as announced only.
