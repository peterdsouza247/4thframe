Games / Tomb Raider

Follow Lara. Choose the adventure that speaks to you.

Tomb Raider follows an archaeologist whose curiosity takes her into places that should have stayed buried. Some games celebrate her confidence; others ask what it costs to become that person. Choose one era first. You do not need three versions of her life as homework.

Our starting recommendation: Start Tomb Raider (2013) for a character-led origin, or Legend for an already confident Lara and a shorter globe-trotting adventure. Choose the first game in I–III Remastered if discovering the series’ original tombs appeals to you. Each is a complete way in.

Choose your way in.

I want a modern origin and a close look at Lara

Tomb Raider (2013)

A first expedition introduces Lara and her friends as people rather than names you should already know. Its survival story gives her later determination an emotional foundation.

Know this much
Lara is a young archaeologist aboard the Endurance. An expedition searching for Yamatai becomes a struggle to get home.
You can skip
All earlier games, the films, comics and animated series. Collectibles and optional tombs are not prerequisites.
The trade-off
Combat and cinematic peril take more space than the older games’ slow exploration. Expect a grittier Lara than the confident figure on the classic covers.
Go next
Continue with Rise of the Tomb Raider, then Shadow of the Tomb Raider. Stop after this game if a single adventure is enough.

I want confident Lara, puzzles and globe-trotting

Tomb Raider: Legend

Its guided opening, fluid movement and supporting cast make a welcoming introduction to the adventurer at work. A personal mystery gives the journey a clear direction.

Know this much
Lara investigates an ancient sword and a mystery connected to her mother. Her team helps remotely; the game introduces them.
You can skip
The six classic games, the Survivor trilogy and reading a family history in advance.
The trade-off
Older camera conventions and quick-time events may take adjustment. The central family mystery continues into Underworld.
Go next
Play Anniversary for this era’s earlier Scion adventure, then Underworld for the convergence. Release order is our recommendation; Anniversary → Legend → Underworld is the era’s broad story order.

I want the original exploration and atmosphere

Tomb Raider (1996), in I–III Remastered

The first adventure lets architecture, silence and dangerous spaces carry the experience. Start with its first game rather than treating the collection as a three-game obligation.

Know this much
Lara accepts a commission to retrieve an ancient artifact called the Scion. Her employer and the relic’s history are part of the mystery.
You can skip
II and III until you want more, the expansions, the later classic games and both reboot trilogies.
The trade-off
Navigation and precise platforming ask for patience. The remaster offers control choices, but the underlying level design remains from the original era.
Go next
Try Tomb Raider II for another mostly standalone relic hunt. Continue into The Last Revelation later if you want the Von Croy story; III is an optional expedition.

I want a self-contained adventure with a friend

Lara Croft and the Guardian of Light

An isometric action-puzzle adventure puts cooperation at the centre. It also works as a solo detour without a larger continuity commitment.

Know this much
Lara and the ancient warrior Totec must deal with a dangerous power released from the Mirror of Smoke.
You can skip
Every mainline game, Lara’s family mysteries and completing all challenge objectives.
The trade-off
Its viewpoint and emphasis differ from the main games. It gives you Lara’s adventure spirit rather than a detailed origin or ongoing character arc.
Go next
Try Lara Croft and the Temple of Osiris for another co-op detour. Choose Legend or Tomb Raider (2013) when you want a mainline story.

Enough to begin.

Same icon, revised histories

The classic games, Legend trilogy and Survivor trilogy originally presented different versions of Lara’s history. Use those eras to understand the released games; do not force every detail into one biography. New projects draw on that legacy, but their final connections are not prerequisites here.

Myth becomes material

Relics are more than collectibles: they can turn legends into immediate dangers. Notice who wants a discovery, what power they expect from it, and who lives with the consequences. These are fictional interpretations of real cultures and myths.

What changes is Lara’s relationship to discovery

Classic Lara often seeks the challenge itself. Legend makes missing family and an old friendship central. Survivor follows the tension between understanding the past, proving herself and protecting people. These are useful lenses, not rules for every scene.

People worth knowing.

Start with who they are and what drives them. Their routes show relevant appearances, not a list of games you must finish. Development notes follow your spoiler setting.

Archaeologist / every era

Lara Croft

Lara’s curiosity is the constant. In the classic games, she is already a capable explorer. Legend gives that drive a family mystery; Survivor begins before her confidence has formed and asks how far she should take the search. Watch what she chooses to protect when knowledge and people compete.

Connections: The supporting relationships belong to their era: Von Croy in the classics, Amanda and Zip in Legend, and Jonah, Sam and Roth in Survivor. Revised histories should not be read as missing chapters you have to fill in.

The bigger picture.

Croft Manor and the family legacy

The manor is a home, a practice space in some games, and a place where Lara’s discoveries accumulate. Different eras give it different emotional weight. Rise’s optional Blood Ties explores the Survivor family history more closely.

Relic hunters and their patrons

Natla’s commission, rival collectors and competing archaeologists turn discovery into a struggle over ownership and use. Ask who funded the expedition and what they expect to gain; it is often more revealing than the relic’s name.

Trinity / Survivor era

A secretive organization seeks ancient power. It becomes the continuing human adversary in Rise and Shadow. You do not need to know its whole history before the 2013 game; the trilogy develops its role.

Yamatai and Kitezh / Survivor era

These lost places connect archaeology with survival and belief. Their histories explain what people protect and what outsiders pursue. Read local accounts as you encounter them rather than needing a lore glossary first.

Paititi and living communities / Survivor era

Shadow places living people alongside the relic hunt. Jonah’s concern and the communities Lara meets make the consequences of an outsider’s search harder to ignore. Their interests matter independently of her mission.

Atlantis, Avalon and Norse myth / Legend era

The Legend trilogy connects relics and mythic places to Lara’s family search. Anniversary supplies the earlier Scion thread; Underworld brings the threads together. The games’ mythological connections are fictional, not a guide to ancient religion.

Sources and scope

Reviewed 3 October 2026. Entry points, optional routes, and play order are Fourth Frame’s editorial recommendations.

Covers the twelve main adventures from 1996 to Shadow of the Tomb Raider, two optional co-op spin-offs, and announced Legacy of Atlantis and Catalyst as orientation only. I–III Remastered and IV–VI Remastered are access routes, not extra story requirements. Unfinished Business, Golden Mask and The Lost Artifact are optional classic expansions; Rise’s Blood Ties and Baba Yaga, Shadow’s DLC tombs, and Underworld’s Beneath the Ashes and Lara’s Shadow are optional deeper dives. Availability of DLC varies by edition and platform; this guide does not require it. Lara Croft GO and other mobile or handheld games, comics, the films and the animated series are optional and not exhaustively indexed. The animated series follows the Survivor trilogy; the live-action films are separate adaptations. Announced games have no outcome recaps, fixed release dates or assigned place in the older era timelines.
