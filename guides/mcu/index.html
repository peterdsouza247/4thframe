Screen / Marvel Cinematic Universe

A shared world. A personal way in.

The MCU is easier to enter when you follow a question, a person, or a corner of its world. Learn what power changes on Earth, why cosmic conflicts reach it, or what heroism means in one neighbourhood.

Our starting recommendation: Start Iron Man for the shared world’s foundation, Guardians of the Galaxy for a largely independent cosmic beginning, or Daredevil season 1 for local, character-driven lore.

Enough to begin.

Power changes responsibility

Armour, experiments, magic, and alien cultures create different kinds of power. The recurring question is who should decide how it is used.

Institutions have motives

Intelligence agencies, governments, companies, and local authorities can protect people and pursue control at the same time.

Connections are optional depth

A standalone story can introduce its immediate conflict. Crossovers gain meaning from the characters you chose to follow, rather than from memorising every reference.

The bigger picture.

S.H.I.E.L.D.

An intelligence organisation trying to respond to unfamiliar threats. Its authority and methods are part of the story’s questions.

HYDRA

An authoritarian movement rooted in Steve’s wartime history. Its later role belongs inside the relevant story reveals.

Asgard and the wider cosmos

Asgard’s culture and other cosmic powers have interests beyond Earth. They connect through characters and objects, not one unified alien agenda.

Infinity Stones

Powerful objects link several early conflicts. Their full consequences are revealed in the crossover recaps.

New York’s competing visions

Matt, Fisk, lawyers, journalists, and neighbourhood communities ask what protecting a city actually requires.
