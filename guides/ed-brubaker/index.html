Ed Brubaker: Where to Start and What to Read Next

By Peter D’Souza
ED BRUBAKER
Fourth Frame
Guide / 5 min

*Start with Pulp for a short complete story, The Fade Out for a complete twelve-issue series, or Criminal: Coward for the beginning of a shared criminal world. If you prefer familiar superheroes, choose Captain America: Winter Soldier*.

Brubaker's independent books are separate from his Marvel and DC continuity. Several major works are collaborations with Sean Phillips; Gotham Central also depends on Greg Rucka and Michael Lark. This guide identifies those boundaries and protects the main endings. Reviewed on 17 October 2026.

Read The Best Ed Brubaker Comics for the critical discussion and our five-writer list for comparisons.

Choose a route

  • One evening, one complete story: Pulp.
  • Hollywood noir: The Fade Out, complete #1–12.
  • An expanding underworld: Criminal, beginning with Coward and following publication order.
  • A recurring investigator: the Reckless graphic novels, in publication order.
  • Superhero espionage: Captain America: Winter Soldier; decide afterwards whether to follow the longer run.
  • Gotham without a superhero viewpoint: all forty issues of Gotham Central.
  • An undercover superhuman thriller: Sleeper, both seasons, with Point Blank as the connected prelude.

Criminal: publication order beats the family tree

Begin with Coward, then Lawless, The Dead and the Dying, Bad Night, The Sinners and The Last of the Innocent. These are the familiar first six standard story collections. They share people and places but offer distinct narrative movements. Following publication order lets connections acquire their intended weight.1

Continue with Wrong Time, Wrong Place, then My Heroes Have Always Been Junkies, Bad Weekend and Cruel Summer if you want the wider project. Those later books have been repackaged in more than one format. Some contain material first published in ordinary Criminal issues; the original graphic novels are not all part of the same numbered run.

Cruel Summer is particularly easy to double-buy: it gathers Criminal (2019) #1 and #5–12. Bad Weekend expands material from that series' #2–3. Deluxe and newer complete formats can also duplicate stories purchased separately. Compare exact contents rather than assuming a new subtitle means entirely new pages.

The broader project has continued beyond the classic numbered shelf, including the 2026 Five Gears in Reverse graphic novel. The route above establishes the core reading path; it does not claim that an older set of trades contains every later publication.2

Avoid arranging the stories by in-world birth dates. A prequel can carry more force when you already know something of a person's future. Coward is a better first purchase than an elaborate chronological spreadsheet.

Pulp and The Fade Out

Pulp: a standalone original graphic novel by Brubaker, Sean Phillips and Jacob Phillips. Standard paperback and hardcover formats tell the story. The Process Edition adds production material; it is an expanded presentation rather than a sequel.3

The Fade Out: read #1–12 in order. The Complete Collection gathers the entire main series, as does the story portion of the deluxe edition. The older three-volume format divides it into Act One, Act Two and Act Three. Choose one sequence. Its adult material includes murder, abuse, exploitation and sexual content.4

Neither book belongs to Criminal's continuity. A familiar creative team is a reason to try them, not a reason to search for a crossover bridge.

Reckless: the initial five-book sequence

Read the first five original graphic novels in publication order:

  1. Reckless.
  2. Friend of the Devil.
  3. Destroy All Monsters.
  4. The Ghost in You.
  5. Follow Me Down.

Each has a main case, while recurring relationships gain meaning across the sequence. These are books, not collections of an otherwise mandatory monthly comic. The Ghost in You shifts attention toward Anna, so publication order also helps preserve the development of the supporting cast.5

This is the initial sequence, not a promise that Ethan's career is permanently complete. You can stop after a case if you want a break. No Criminal, Pulp or Fade Out reading is required first.

Captain America: a focused opening

Choose Captain America: Winter Soldier Ultimate Collection, containing Captain America (2005) #1–9 and #11–14. Some editions divide the story into two smaller volumes. Issue #10 is omitted from this particular collection; the missing number is not evidence that your copy is defective.6

That opening gives you enough of the style and premise to decide whether to pursue Brubaker's much longer Captain America phase. Beyond it, follow the collected sequence through Red Menace, the Death of Captain America movement, The Man with No Face, Road to Reborn, and Captain America: Reborn. Later Captain America, Captain America and Bucky and Winter Soldier titles introduce renumbering and branch choices, so the full career is a separate, larger project.

For this author guide, the opening Winter Soldier story is a deliberate stopping point. It does not promise the completion of every subsequent Captain America storyline. The film is an adaptation, not a replacement for reading earlier or later comic chapters.

Gotham Central, Catwoman and Sleeper

Gotham Central: begin #1 and continue to #40, using the omnibus or a full numbered book sequence. Brubaker and Rucka divide writing responsibilities, and the series continues beyond Brubaker's portion. Read the whole if the ensemble interests you; calling all forty issues a Brubaker solo run would miscredit the work.7

Catwoman: begin with Trail of the Catwoman. The 2012 Vol. 1 gathers Catwoman #1–9, the Detective Comics #759–762 backups and Selina's Big Score. That last story is written and drawn by Darwyn Cooke. If you own it separately, this volume duplicates it. Later formats with similar names can have different contents. Brubaker's era continues through further trades, with artists including Cameron Stewart; you can try the opening before committing.8

Sleeper: the main story is Season One #1–12 → Season Two #1–12. Point Blank #1–5, drawn by Colin Wilson, is the prelude. Read it first for context if your edition includes it, or begin Sleeper itself and return to the prelude later. A full omnibus can be simpler than assembling separate editions. This is a WildStorm setting, separate from Batman's Gotham; no full Authority reading project is required.

Horror branches and material to expect

Fatale runs twenty-four issues and has a complete main story. Kill or Be Killed runs twenty issues and is also complete. Both are Brubaker/Phillips projects with Elizabeth Breitweiser's colour. Read each from its own #1, or choose its complete collection; neither is a sequel to the other.

Across the independent work, expect violence, coercion, sexual material, addiction and unreliable accounts of events. The exact emphasis varies. For a shorter introduction with a definite endpoint, take Pulp. For an expanding setting, take Coward. Those choices avoid buying a large edition before discovering whether this kind of noir is what you want.
