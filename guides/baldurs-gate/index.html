Games / Baldur’s Gate

Gather your party. Start where you belong.

Baldur’s Gate has two useful beginnings: a continuous classic saga and a much later adventure that welcomes new players. Knowing every Forgotten Realms name is unnecessary.

Our starting recommendation: Start Baldur’s Gate 3 for a modern, self-contained party adventure. Begin Baldur’s Gate: Enhanced Edition if you want to follow the original protagonist across games.

Choose your way in.

I want the modern party adventure

Baldur’s Gate 3

A new central conflict and a largely new cast make the third game a welcoming starting point. Returning people reward familiarity rather than require it.

Know this much
Your party is infected with mind-flayer parasites and needs answers.
You can skip
BG1–2, Siege of Dragonspear, Forgotten Realms novels, and tabletop campaigns.
The trade-off
Returning-character history will carry less personal weight. You can learn it later without losing the main story.
Go next
Follow companion quests and the main acts. Try the classic saga afterwards if you want Jaheira and Minsc’s earlier adventures.

I want one character’s complete classic journey

Baldur’s Gate: Enhanced Edition

The first game introduces your ward, the Sword Coast, and a mystery that grows into the next game’s personal conflict.

Know this much
You have grown up at Candlekeep under Gorion’s protection.
You can skip
BG3 and all external D&D reading.
The trade-off
Low-level characters are fragile and the older rules need adjustment. Use pause and a comfortable difficulty.
Go next
Continue to Shadows of Amn, then Throne of Bhaal. Tales of the Sword Coast and Siege of Dragonspear are optional detours.

I want the classic saga’s richer party stories

Baldur’s Gate II: Shadows of Amn

A concentrated opening and more developed companion quests make it a workable direct entry. The game lets you create a new character.

Know this much
You are a child of Bhaal whose earlier adventure has attracted dangerous attention.
You can skip
A complete BG1 playthrough if its slower opening is a barrier.
The trade-off
The opening reveals the first game’s central identity mystery. Read the optional recap only if you accept that reveal.
Go next
Continue to Throne of Bhaal for the protagonist’s conclusion.

Enough to begin.

The Sword Coast

A region of cities, wilderness, competing faiths, and political ambitions in the Forgotten Realms.

Two generations of rules

BG1–2 use older Dungeons & Dragons systems with pausable real-time combat. BG3 uses turn-based combat inspired by fifth edition.

Choices are personal

Companion survival, loyalty, romance, and the protagonist’s future can vary. This guide does not declare one playthrough correct.

The bigger picture.

Bhaal and the Dead Three

Their cults connect murder, domination, and death to political schemes. BG3 teaches the immediate stakes in its own story.

Harpers

A loose network concerned with resisting dangerous concentrations of power; Jaheira offers a personal perspective.

Mind flayers and githyanki

Their histories explain parts of the parasite crisis, but the games supply the necessary context.

Sources and scope

Reviewed 3 October 2026. Entry points, optional routes, and play order are Fourth Frame’s editorial recommendations.

Covers the original saga, its major story expansions, and BG3. Enhanced Editions are versions rather than extra chronology entries. The Black Pits arena stories and console Dark Alliance games are outside this character route. BG3 shares a world with tabletop material such as Descent into Avernus, but reading that adventure is optional.
