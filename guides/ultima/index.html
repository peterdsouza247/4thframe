Games / Ultima

Where to start Ultima, and what makes Britannia worth exploring.

Start with Ultima VII: The Black Gate if you want to explore a country full of people with jobs, grievances and suspiciously helpful advice. Choose Ultima IV if you want to understand the Avatar, or Underworld if a first-person dungeon sounds more inviting. There is no need to finish three early computer RPGs before reaching the part that interests you.

Our starting recommendation: For a first visit, play The Black Gate, then Serpent Isle. For the moral foundations of Britannia, take IV → V → VI. Underworld I → II is a separate playing route; Online is an MMO, not the next chapter after IX.

Choose the kind of adventure you want.

Britannia supplies the central history. Underworld approaches it from inside a dungeon, while the other branches change the setting or the way you play. These groups keep the timeline useful without turning every release into homework.

Choose your way in.

I want the strongest introduction to the world

Ultima VII: The Black Gate

A murder in Trinsic gives you a reason to question people and follow leads. Soon you are comparing what an organisation promises with how its members behave. Exploring houses, reading books and checking an apparently minor conversation are a large part of the game.

Know this much
You are the Avatar, a visitor from Earth and an established friend of Lord British. Iolo and the other companions know you already. Accept that history as background; the investigation starts here.
You can skip
Ultima I–VI, both Worlds of Ultima games and Underworld. The earlier-story recap is optional.
The trade-off
Party combat is untidy and feeding people can become a chore. The attraction is the investigation and the amount of ordinary life surrounding it. Use the PC game for this route; the SNES adaptation is substantially different.
Go next
Continue with Serpent Isle. Underworld II fits between the two if you want an additional Guardian story in a different style.

I want to understand why this series matters

Ultima IV: Quest of the Avatar

IV makes your conduct the central objective. Paying honestly, helping people and knowing when to show restraint contribute to becoming the Avatar. V then asks what happens when a ruler turns those ideals into punishable laws. That is a good reason to play two very old RPGs.

Know this much
Britannia has emerged from earlier wars. Lord British wants an example of how to live, and your character must become worthy of that responsibility.
You can skip
I–III and their villains. You can learn that history later without weakening IV’s purpose.
The trade-off
Expect a command reference, typed conversation keywords and your own notes. Some moral tasks can feel mechanical once you understand their rules. Begin here because the idea interests you, rather than because the numbering says you must.
Go next
Play V, then VI. Together they develop the virtues through coercion, cultural conflict and the limits of the hero’s own assumptions.

I want a first-person dungeon RPG

Ultima Underworld: The Stygian Abyss

You enter an underground colony that has fallen apart. Its survivors have different loyalties and useful information; finding a way through involves conversation, supplies and a gradually improving understanding of the place. The dungeon becomes familiar as you learn its connections.

Know this much
You have been blamed for a kidnapping. Finding the missing girl in the Abyss is your way to prove your innocence. Earlier Ultima plots are unnecessary.
You can skip
Every numbered game. Underworld provides a self-contained problem and enough context to solve it.
The trade-off
The original interface predates familiar modern first-person controls. Learn turning, looking, attacking and using objects before judging the whole game by your first encounter. Jumping remains awkward.
Go next
Play Underworld II for more varied worlds and a stronger connection to Britannia. Its opening follows The Black Gate, whose conclusion it can spoil.

I enjoy early computer RPGs and want the foundations

Ultima III: Exodus

III gives you a four-person party to build and tactical battles to prepare for. Its choices of classes, supplies and exploration make a more focused introduction to early Ultima than II’s sprawling time travel.

Know this much
Earlier adventurers defeated Mondain and Minax. A new threat named Exodus now endangers Sosaria.
You can skip
I and II unless their historical importance is part of the attraction. Their recaps supply enough context.
The trade-off
Its appeal depends on enjoying old interfaces and working out how a world operates with limited guidance. Later Ultimas offer much more convincing societies and character writing.
Go next
Move to IV. Return to I for a shorter look at the series’ beginnings; leave II until you know you want the detour.

I want a shared world with crafting and other players

Ultima Online

Online builds its appeal around people making a life in Britannia: learning skills, trading, adventuring and joining a community. Choosing a shard and understanding its rules matter more than knowing the numbered games’ endings.

Know this much
A shard is a server world. This is a separate multiplayer continuity with its own updates and rules, not a single-player sequel.
You can skip
The complete Avatar saga. You do not have to earn the title in IV before making an Online character.
The trade-off
Access rules differ between the Endless Journey option and subscriptions. Housing and some other systems have restrictions. Check the official account comparison and the rules of the shard you choose.
Go next
Use the official beginner material, choose a shard and find a community that suits your pace. Treat New Legacy as a distinct ruleset, not an exact restoration of 1997.

Enough to begin.

What playing Ultima actually involves

A conversation may give you a person’s name, a place or a word to ask somebody else about. Follow that lead, write down what changes your understanding, and return when you have something useful to ask. In the earlier games, the manual and map are part of learning to play. A quest journal cannot remember a clue for you if the game never had one.

The Avatar and the virtues

The player character begins as a stranger in another world. From IV, becoming the Avatar means embodying eight virtues: honesty, compassion, valour, justice, sacrifice, honour, spirituality and humility. Their practical meaning changes across the games. V is interested in coercion; VI questions the assumption that the apparent enemy must be wrong.

Sosaria, Britannia and returning companions

Sosaria is the early setting; Britannia is the kingdom familiar from IV onwards. Lord British is its ruler. Iolo, Shamino and Dupre are recurring friends, which is why VII can begin with familiar greetings. Your character has a past here even when you, the player, do not.

Which Ultima VII edition to choose

The Complete Edition contains The Black Gate and Serpent Isle, with Forge of Virtue and The Silver Seed respectively. Serpent Isle is a full second adventure, not a small optional mission pack. Play Black Gate first. Forge of Virtue belongs inside its campaign; Silver Seed belongs inside Serpent Isle. You can leave both expansions for later without losing the central plot.

Running VII with Exult

Exult is a community engine for the PC versions of VII and Serpent Isle. It needs your legally acquired game data and offers modern conveniences. Follow its own setup documentation, keep separate saves when changing engines, and check whether you are installing optional content changes as well. A new engine does not make the original combat or inventory design disappear.

What the November 2026 releases change

EA brought the numbered PC games and both Underworld titles to Steam on 5 November 2026. Its launch announcement lists these Ultimas as faithful ports rather than the restored Anniversary editions given to selected Wing Commander games. Check each store’s system details before buying. A new store listing does not by itself mean redesigned controls.

A few habits that save frustration

Keep rotating saves, including one before a major journey or experiment. Carry food, check what your companions are carrying and make brief notes about unresolved conversations. If you need help, look up the specific obstacle before opening a full walkthrough. The next paragraph may answer a mystery you had not reached yet.

How the branches fit

Underworld I can stand alone. Underworld II follows Black Gate and precedes Serpent Isle in the story. Savage Empire and Martian Dreams use VI’s foundations for separate adventures beyond Britannia. Online has its own continuity. The story-group sort keeps those routes together; it does not assign exact dates to every side trip.

Worlds of Ultima: The Savage Empire

The Avatar arrives in Eodon, a valley of dinosaurs and rival tribes threatened by the Myrmidex.

The bigger picture.

Britannia and the shrines

The towns and shrines express the virtues in different ways. Their ideals make a useful starting point for questions, rather than proof that every citizen behaves well.

The Fellowship

An organisation offering community and a coherent answer to spiritual dissatisfaction in VII. Investigate its members and activities before looking up its secrets.

The gargoyles

A people with their own principles and history. VI becomes more interesting when you listen to their account rather than treating their appearance as an explanation.

The Ophidians

A civilisation whose ideas about Order, Chaos and Balance underlie Serpent Isle. Learn the terms as the investigation introduces them; solving the entire cosmology before starting would spoil much of the discovery.

Pagan’s elemental powers

Religious and magical authority on Pagan is organised around forces that affect ordinary life. Learning rituals offers a way forward while raising questions about the price of escape.
