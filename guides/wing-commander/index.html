Games / Wing Commander

Where to start Wing Commander, from the Tiger’s Claw to Privateer.

The appeal is easy to understand: fly a dangerous mission, return to the carrier and find out what the people beside you make of it. Wing Commander gives the pilots names and lets the war follow them into the bar. Start with III for the cinematic campaigns, the original for the full history, or Privateer if you would rather own the ship and choose the work.

Our starting recommendation: For a manageable first route, play III: Heart of the Tiger, then IV: The Price of Freedom. Take I → II → III → IV for Blair’s longer story. Choose Privateer for trading and ship progression, or Prophecy for a later combat-focused starting point.

A carrier berth, or a ship of your own.

The main campaigns follow a military career. Privateer changes your relationship with the universe, while Academy and Armada experiment with how the battles are arranged. You can choose one route without buying the whole catalogue.

Choose your way in.

I want a space opera with a memorable crew

Wing Commander III: Heart of the Tiger

The TCS Victory gives Blair a new posting and the player a cast to get to know. Briefings, conversations and flight missions keep the war connected to individual people. Mark Hamill’s Blair is a useful point of entry even if you have never seen the earlier sprite-based games.

Know this much
Humanity’s Terran Confederation has fought the Kilrathi for decades. Blair is an experienced pilot; Paladin and Maniac are old acquaintances, and Hobbes is a Kilrathi ally serving with the Confederation.
You can skip
I, II and their mission packs. The optional earlier-story recap explains Blair’s reputation without requiring several campaigns.
The trade-off
This remains a space-combat game. Conversations provide context and choices, but you will spend a great deal of time learning to fight from a cockpit. It does not offer Mass Effect’s on-foot exploration or character builds.
Go next
Play IV next. Its argument about loyalty and military power benefits from knowing the people and institutions in III.

I want the full carrier-campaign history

Wing Commander

The original establishes the routine: listen to the briefing, fly with a wingman and return to a crew that reacts to the fighting. Different campaign paths and possible wingman losses make success feel larger than your own score.

Know this much
You are a young Confederation pilot aboard the Tiger’s Claw during the Kilrathi war. The personal name Christopher Blair is established in the later games.
You can skip
Novels, the film and the animated series. They are not prerequisites for the game’s campaign.
The trade-off
Sprite-based enemies and a cockpit full of instruments take adjustment. The original’s responsiveness to losses also differs from the more tightly scripted stories that follow.
Go next
Continue with II, then III and IV. Play The Secret Missions and Crusade after I if you want more; they are harder optional continuations.

I want trading, freedom and a ship to improve

Wing Commander: Privateer

Your starting ship and finances determine which jobs you can sensibly accept. A successful run buys equipment that changes your next decision. The main plot is available when you want it, while the everyday business of surviving gives the universe its appeal.

Know this much
You are Grayson Burrows, an independent pilot in Gemini. The Confederation and Kilrathi conflict is background pressure rather than a campaign you must have completed.
You can skip
Every numbered Wing Commander. Their saves and character histories are unnecessary.
The trade-off
Early earnings can be slow and encounters can be punishing. Repeated journeys are part of the progression. Buying a better ship is satisfying partly because the first one makes its limitations clear.
Go next
Play Righteous Fire after the base story. Try Privateer 2 later if its unusual setting appeals; it is not Burrows’s next adventure.

I mainly want the later dogfighting

Wing Commander: Prophecy

A new protagonist and carrier let you learn alongside another generation of pilots. The later engine supports fluid combat and larger-looking engagements, making this a useful entry if the older games’ presentation is the main obstacle.

Know this much
The Kilrathi war is over. Lance Casey joins the Midway, and Christopher Blair is an established senior figure. You can accept those facts without reading the earlier endings.
You can skip
I–IV and Privateer. Familiarity adds weight to returning characters, but Casey’s campaign explains its immediate threat.
The trade-off
The crew drama and enemy are less distinctive than the conflicts in III and IV. Starting here also tells you the broad historical outcome of the Kilrathi war.
Go next
Continue with Secret Ops. Return to III → IV if you want more of the older cast; expect the presentation and flight model to change.

I am most interested in the political thriller

Wing Commander IV: The Price of Freedom

Suspicious attacks threaten a fragile peace. Blair must judge orders and evidence while serving an institution with its own interests. This is the strongest route for someone more interested in the argument surrounding the battles than the history of the Kilrathi war.

Know this much
Blair is a veteran, Tolwyn a powerful senior officer, and Paladin an old ally. The war has ended, but its military habits and political consequences remain.
You can skip
I–II. III is recommended first, although the optional recap can supply its central outcome if you choose to begin here.
The trade-off
The flight mechanics build on III more than they reinvent it, and difficult missions can interrupt the story’s pace. Starting here sacrifices some of the meaning attached to familiar faces.
Go next
Choose Prophecy for the next main campaign. Play III afterwards only if knowing its ending will not undermine your interest.

Enough to begin.

What happens between missions

Visit the people available on the carrier before reporting for the next flight. Their conversations establish disagreements and relationships that a mission briefing cannot. Later games use full-motion video, usually shortened to FMV, for these scenes. The attraction depends on caring about the person whose voice you hear over the radio.

How the flying works

These are accessible military space-combat simulations, but they still ask you to read instruments. Learn target selection, radar, speed control, afterburners and the communications menu. Practise matching an enemy’s movement rather than holding the trigger as soon as a silhouette appears. Check the reference card for the particular game; controls and equipment vary.

Wingmen and objectives

Issue wingman orders and watch the objective as well as your target. Chasing a fighter far from an escort can win a small engagement while losing the mission. Bombers and capital-ship attacks have their own requirements in the games that use them. Read the briefing before blaming your guns for a problem they were not designed to solve.

Failure and campaign branches

The original can continue along different paths after poor results, and wingmen can be lost. Later entries use more scripted story beats alongside mission performance and dialogue decisions. A successful replay is a valid choice, but constant reloading is not the only way to see the campaign. Keep separate saves if you want to compare outcomes.

Which route carries the main story

I → II → III → IV follows Blair from the Tiger’s Claw through the war and its aftermath. Prophecy changes the player character to Casey; Secret Ops follows Prophecy. The mission packs sit after their parent campaigns. Privateer follows an independent pilot, and Privateer 2 uses another cast and setting.

What the Anniversary labels mean

EA released Anniversary editions of I, II, III, IV, Prophecy and Privateer 2 on 5 November 2026. The announced changes vary: I–II integrate community compatibility work and Kilrathi Saga versions; the later cinematic games receive restored footage and other enhancements. Prophecy includes Secret Ops. Privateer, Academy and Armada are listed as faithful ports. Consult the edition notes for your chosen game rather than assuming one feature list covers all nine.

Choosing controls and a first session

Use the launcher’s supported input options and the game’s reference card. A joystick can suit sustained flying, but do not buy one before checking whether the available mouse or controller setup works for you. Spend a short practice session learning to select targets, issue an order and return to the carrier. Your first campaign mission is a poor place to discover that you never mapped the command you need.

Privateer’s opening hours

Keep money for repairs and supplies instead of spending every credit on a purchase. Compare a contract’s destination with what your ship can survive. Save before committing to a risky journey, and pay attention to how shields, guns and equipment draw on your resources. The upgrade loop is the point; the main plot can wait while you establish yourself.

Academy, Armada and the adaptations

Academy is a custom-mission simulator, not the animated series of the same name. Armada combines ship-to-ship action with a light strategic campaign and multiplayer. Super Wing Commander is a remake of the original with revised presentation and campaign material. Arena is a later arcade offshoot. None is a missing numbered chapter you must complete before IV.

The pilot followed through I–IV

Christopher Blair

He wants to serve effectively while dealing with reputation, personal loyalty and the judgement of his superiors. The games move him from a young pilot to someone whose decisions carry political weight.

Connections: Angel, Paladin and Maniac give his career continuity. Tolwyn is a powerful authority whose approval and priorities matter.

The bigger picture.

Terran Confederation

The human-led state and military in which the main pilots serve. Its need to win a war does not settle every argument about how it should act.

Kilrathi Empire

A feline alien civilisation with a powerful imperial military and ruling clans. Individual Kilrathi can have different loyalties; the games do not require you to treat every member as interchangeable.

Union of Border Worlds

A political grouping central to IV’s post-war conflict. Its relationship with the Confederation raises questions about whose security and independence receive priority.

Gemini’s independent operators

Merchants, mercenaries, pirates and other groups make Privateer’s space a place of work. Their attitudes help determine how safe a profitable route really is.

The Tri-System

Privateer 2’s trading region has its own institutions and criminal interests. Its distinct setting is why the title’s sequel number is a poor explanation of what to expect.

The new threat in Prophecy

An unfamiliar enemy changes the strategic situation after the Kilrathi war. Let the campaign introduce its methods and history; an encyclopaedia search will reveal more than the opening briefing does.
