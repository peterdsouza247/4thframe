Games / The Legend of Zelda

Choose an adventure. The timeline can wait.

Most Zelda games introduce a new adventure with familiar names and themes. Their historical connections are optional context, not a requirement to work through every incarnation of Link before choosing one.

Our starting recommendation: Start Breath of the Wild for open-ended exploration, Link’s Awakening for a compact traditional adventure, or Ocarina of Time for the classic 3D structure. Echoes of Wisdom offers a welcoming adventure led by Zelda herself.

Choose your way in.

I want freedom and discovery

Breath of the Wild

Its world teaches through exploration and experimentation. Local people and optional memories provide context without requiring earlier games.

Know this much
Hyrule is living in the aftermath of a calamity, and Link must decide how to confront the danger still present.
You can skip
Every earlier Zelda, collecting every shrine or seed, and a timeline explanation.
The trade-off
Its open structure is different from the older item-and-dungeon pattern. The story is partly discovered out of order.
Go next
Continue to Tears of the Kingdom if you want more of this Link and Zelda. Hyrule Warriors stories are optional.

I want a compact, traditional adventure

Link’s Awakening

A self-contained island mystery introduces Zelda-style dungeons and tools without requiring knowledge of Hyrule’s royal history.

Know this much
Link washes ashore on Koholint and searches for a way home.
You can skip
The earlier games, their lore, and a branch-by-branch marathon.
The trade-off
Its smaller world and older puzzle structure are less open-ended than Breath of the Wild. The original and remake tell the same central story.
Go next
Try A Link to the Past or A Link Between Worlds for a larger top-down adventure.

I want the classic 3D adventure

Ocarina of Time

A focused opening, expanding tools, and memorable companions introduce the series’ traditional 3D pattern. It also supplies useful context for later branch discussions.

Know this much
A boy from the forest is drawn into Hyrule’s struggle over sacred power.
You can skip
The earlier released games and learning the official timeline before playing.
The trade-off
Camera, combat, and presentation depend on the edition. Its centrality to the timeline does not make it compulsory for newer games.
Go next
Choose Majora’s Mask for this Link’s direct follow-up, or The Wind Waker and Twilight Princess for different later branches.

I want to play as Zelda

Echoes of Wisdom

Its new central mechanic offers inventive solutions in a compact top-down Hyrule. A fresh crisis introduces its own supporting cast.

Know this much
Strange rifts threaten the kingdom; Zelda works with a companion called Tri.
You can skip
Earlier Zelda games, playing as Link first, and knowing its precise timeline position.
The trade-off
Echo-based problem solving differs from the usual sword-first approach. Familiar geography is a bonus rather than a prerequisite.
Go next
Try Link’s Awakening for another compact adventure or Breath of the Wild for a larger playground.

Enough to begin.

Recurring names, different lives

Link and Zelda usually refer to different people across distant eras. Familiar names do not mean every game follows one uninterrupted lifetime.

An adventure first

Exploration, puzzles, tools, and local characters explain each game’s immediate stakes. You can learn its version of Hyrule as you play.

Several historical branches

Nintendo’s timeline divides after Ocarina of Time. Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom occupy a distant era; this guide does not invent a definitive merger with an earlier branch.

The bigger picture.

Hyrule’s peoples

Hylians, Zora, Gorons, Gerudo, Rito, and other communities have histories that vary between games. Their local introductions are enough to begin.

Royal and sacred traditions

The Triforce, goddess traditions, and Master Sword recur, but a game usually explains the version relevant to its plot.

Allies across realms

Companions such as Midna and Tri make individual adventures distinct. Learning them as you meet them is part of the experience.

Warriors campaigns

Age of Calamity follows altered events; Age of Imprisonment is an optional canonical ancient-era story connected to Tears. Their different relationships should not be collapsed into one label.

Sources and scope

Reviewed 3 October 2026. Entry points, optional routes, and play order are Fourth Frame’s editorial recommendations.

Covers the main adventure series from the original game through Echoes of Wisdom, including the Oracle pair, Four Sword games, and Tri Force Heroes. Remakes and the announced Ocarina anniversary edition retell existing adventures. Age of Calamity and Age of Imprisonment are optional related campaigns with different continuity relationships; the latter’s coverage is introductory. Other Warriors editions, crossovers, and minor spin-offs are outside this route. Nintendo’s timeline places Echoes after Tri Force Heroes and before the original game. The distant-era placement remains explicitly separate.
