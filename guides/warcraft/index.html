Games / Warcraft

Find your way into Azeroth.

Choose the adventure you want. The strategy campaigns, modern MMO, and Classic offer different ways into the same world. You can enjoy any of them without completing decades of games.

Our starting recommendation: For a focused character story, begin with Warcraft III. For the MMO, follow modern WoW’s new-player path and join the current campaign. Use the recaps to fill gaps as they become relevant.

Choose your way in.

I want a story with memorable characters

Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos

Its tutorial and connected campaigns introduce the people whose choices shape later Warcraft. It supplies a satisfying beginning without two older games of preparation.

Know this much
Orcs came from Draenor, lost a war against the Alliance, and now seek a future on Azeroth. The Legion is a demonic threat.
You can skip
Warcraft I and II, WoW, novels, and comics. Read their short recaps only if you become curious.
The trade-off
This is a strategy game with armies and missions. It introduces the world through its leaders rather than a custom adventurer.
Go next
Play The Frozen Throne next. Stop there for a complete strategy-era journey, or follow one character into WoW.

I want an MMO I can join today

Modern World of Warcraft

The current new-player experience teaches your class and leads through a modern introduction. You can reach today’s shared adventure without clearing every old zone or raid.

Know this much
Start with the new-player path offered in game, including Dragonflight where directed. Alliance and Horde are coalitions; Azeroth faces threats larger than either.
You can skip
Clearing old expansions in release order, collecting everything, and buying lore books.
The trade-off
The MMO tells stories across quests, cinematics, and raids. Old campaigns are not one seamless sequence, and access to the latest expansion is separate from learning the world.
Go next
Join Midnight when your character is ready. Use the saga recap below; choose The War Within first if you want the arc’s beginning.

I want to follow the current story from its beginning

The War Within

It starts the Worldsoul Saga and makes Azeroth’s own fate the focus. A short character recap is a smaller commitment than replaying the previous ten expansions.

Know this much
Azeroth has a world-soul. Alleria uses the Void, Anduin is recovering from trauma, and Xal’atath is gathering forces.
You can skip
Full playthroughs of Battle for Azeroth, Shadowlands, and Dragonflight. Their recaps supply the relevant bridge.
The trade-off
This is still WoW: character requirements and campaign access apply. A lore entry point is not a promise that a fresh character can immediately enter every quest.
Go next
Follow the main campaign, then Midnight. Return to older regions only when their stories interest you.

I want to discover a world slowly

World of Warcraft Classic

Local quests and travel let you learn Azeroth through your own adventurer. Prior knowledge of Warcraft III is useful, but unnecessary.

Know this much
The factions are rebuilding after major wars. Pick a race and class that interest you and learn their home region.
You can skip
Strategy games, modern expansion campaigns, and a checklist of raids.
The trade-off
Classic’s rules and pace differ from modern WoW. Choose the Classic version your friends play; it is not a required first step before retail.
Go next
Continue through the zones you enjoy, or move to modern WoW later with a short lore recap.

Enough to begin.

Azeroth and Draenor

Azeroth is the main world. Draenor is the orcs’ original home; a portal connects their histories.

Alliance and Horde

These are changing coalitions of peoples, not permanent labels for good and evil. They fight each other and sometimes cooperate.

Legion, Scourge, and Void

The Legion is a demonic invading force; the Scourge is an undead army; the Void represents a different cosmic danger. Their stories overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

The story, at your pace.

Titles and starting premises are visible. Story outcomes stay hidden until you choose to reveal them. You can play a recommended route without completing this timeline.

Warcraft’s release and journey order broadly align. Warlords of Draenor is an alternate-history detour after Pandaria, not the original past. The starting-route filter highlights Warcraft III and the Worldsoul Saga; older expansions remain optional context.

1994

The First War · Released

Warcraft: Orcs & Humans

An invading orc army crosses a magical gateway into the human kingdom of Stormwind.

Where it fits: Recap it unless you enjoy early strategy games. The portal and the fall of Stormwind are the useful foundations.

Story outcomes: Warcraft: Orcs & Humans (spoilers)

Stormwind falls. Survivors flee north, turning a local invasion into a threat that other kingdoms must answer. Later Warcraft stories establish this outcome; the original two campaigns offer incompatible victories.

1995

The Second War · Released

Warcraft II: Tides of Darkness

Human kingdoms and their allies unite against the expanding Horde.

Where it fits: A good optional visit for military history, naval campaigns, and the origins of the Alliance.

Story outcomes: Warcraft II: Tides of Darkness (spoilers)

The Alliance defeats the Horde. The surviving orcs face captivity, while the struggle reaches beyond a single human kingdom.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

1996

The expedition to Draenor · Released

Warcraft II: Beyond the Dark Portal

An Alliance expedition crosses the portal to confront danger on the orcs’ homeworld.

Where it fits: This explains Outland and why Khadgar and Alleria have histories far from home. A recap is enough for most newcomers.

Story outcomes: Warcraft II: Beyond the Dark Portal (spoilers)

Ner’zhul opens additional portals and shatters Draenor. The expedition closes the connection to Azeroth, leaving its heroes stranded beyond it. The broken world becomes Outland.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2002

The Third War · Released

Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos

A spreading plague, a demonic invasion, and rival peoples force Azeroth’s leaders to choose who they can trust.

Where it fits: The strongest introduction to Warcraft’s recurring characters. Play the tutorial and campaigns in their intended order.

Story outcomes: Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos (spoilers)

Arthas takes Frostmourne and becomes a death knight, destroying the kingdom he meant to protect. Thrall builds a new Horde. Humans, orcs, and night elves unite at Mount Hyjal to defeat Archimonde. Sylvanas is killed and raised into undeath.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2003

After the Third War · Released

Warcraft III: The Frozen Throne

The survivors of the war pursue competing futures across Azeroth and Outland.

Where it fits: Play after Reign of Chaos. It is the bridge to WoW and several of its major character arcs.

Story outcomes: Warcraft III: The Frozen Throne (spoilers)

Sylvanas breaks free and leads the Forsaken. Illidan establishes himself in Outland, then loses to Arthas at Icecrown. Arthas reaches the Frozen Throne and becomes the Lich King. Rexxar’s campaign helps establish the Horde’s home in Durotar.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2004

A world of adventurers · Released

World of Warcraft

Years after the Third War, adventurers explore a fragile world whose local dangers hide larger threats.

Where it fits: Choose Classic for the slower discovery of a world. You do not need to finish its raids before playing modern WoW.

Story outcomes: World of Warcraft (spoilers)

The Alliance and Horde consolidate their communities while adventurers confront threats including Ragnaros, the black dragonflight, the qiraji, and the Scourge. There is no single compulsory campaign connecting every zone.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2007

Outland · Released

The Burning Crusade

The Dark Portal reopens, bringing adventurers to a ruined world contested by demons and rival rulers.

Where it fits: An optional follow-up to Illidan’s Warcraft III story. Its finale is separate from the Black Temple arc.

Story outcomes: The Burning Crusade (spoilers)

Illidan is defeated at the Black Temple. Kael’thas’s alliance with the Legion leads to a confrontation at the Sunwell, where Kil’jaeden’s arrival is stopped and the Sunwell is restored.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2008

Northrend · Released

Wrath of the Lich King

The campaign against the Scourge takes the factions to the frozen north.

Where it fits: The most direct WoW continuation of Arthas’s story. For this character route, Warcraft III matters more than playing every earlier expansion.

Story outcomes: Wrath of the Lich King (spoilers)

Arthas is defeated at Icecrown Citadel. Bolvar Fordragon takes the helm to contain the Scourge, showing that killing its ruler does not simply erase the undead threat.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2010

A shattered Azeroth · Released

Cataclysm

Deathwing’s return transforms familiar lands and draws the dragon Aspects into a struggle for survival.

Where it fits: Recap the world’s transformation. Explore it if the altered zones or dragon history interest you.

Story outcomes: Cataclysm (spoilers)

Deathwing is defeated with the aid of Thrall and the dragon Aspects. Azeroth survives, but its geography and the dragons’ role have changed.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2012

Pandaria and the faction war · Released

Mists of Pandaria

The Alliance and Horde bring their conflict to a land where destructive emotions can become tangible threats.

Where it fits: A strong optional regional adventure. The Garrosh recap is the bridge to Warlords of Draenor.

Story outcomes: Mists of Pandaria (spoilers)

Garrosh’s rule drives a rebellion within the Horde. Alliance forces and Horde rebels defeat him in the Siege of Orgrimmar. Pandaria’s sha make the cost of the faction war visible.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2014

An alternate Draenor · Released

Warlords of Draenor

An alternate version of the orcs’ homeworld becomes the staging ground for an Iron Horde invasion.

Where it fits: Recap this for Legion. This is an alternate-history branch reached after Pandaria, not a replacement for the original First War.

Story outcomes: Warlords of Draenor (spoilers)

Garrosh’s intervention creates a different path for Draenor’s clans. The Iron Horde gives way to a Legion threat; this timeline’s Gul’dan ultimately reaches the main Azeroth.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2016

The Legion’s return · Released

Legion

A renewed demonic invasion sends Azeroth’s defenders to the Broken Isles and beyond.

Where it fits: A substantial optional story for Illidan and the Legion. Its aftermath explains Azeroth’s wound and the next faction conflict.

Story outcomes: Legion (spoilers)

The campaign reaches Argus. The Legion’s leadership is overcome and Sargeras is imprisoned, but his sword wounds Azeroth. Illidan remains behind as Sargeras’s jailer. Anduin inherits the throne after Varian’s death.

Story outcomes hidden. Choose a completed game above to unlock them.

2018

War and a wounded world · Released

Battle for Azeroth

The factions seek powerful allies while the consequences of Azeroth’s wound fuel another war.

Where it fits: Recap the faction rupture and Azeroth’s wound before the Worldsoul Saga. Play the regions if naval kingdoms and faction politics appeal to you.

Story outcomes: Battle for Azeroth (spoilers)

Sylvanas burns Teldrassil, and the war divides the Horde as well as the factions. Her rule ends after Saurfang’s challenge. The campaign also confronts Azshara and N’Zoth, while Xal’atath gains freedom from her dagger.

2020

Beyond the veil · Released

Shadowlands

A breach between the living world and the afterlife draws Azeroth’s heroes into the realms of the dead.

Where it fits: A short recap supplies the character context for Anduin and Sylvanas. It is not required homework before modern WoW.

Story outcomes: Shadowlands (spoilers)

The Jailer’s plan is defeated and Anduin is freed from domination. Sylvanas is sentenced to recover souls from the Maw. Anduin’s experiences leave lasting wounds that matter when he returns in The War Within.

The Dragon Isles · Released

Dragonflight

The dragonflights return to their ancestral home as old enemies challenge their legacy.

Where it fits: A welcoming modern leveling introduction. Follow its main campaign and leave completion of every reputation and side quest for later.

Story outcomes: Dragonflight (spoilers)

The dragonflights resist the Primal Incarnates. Amirdrassil survives Fyrakk’s assault and the Aspects regain their power through Azeroth. The story restores a sense of home while threats around Iridikron point toward the Void.

The War Within

A call from Azeroth draws adventurers beneath the surface to Khaz Algar, where Xal’atath is gathering allies.

The bigger picture.

Alliance

A coalition whose human, dwarven, elven, and other members do not always agree. Follow its internal arguments as well as its war with the Horde.

Horde

A coalition shaped by displacement, survival, and arguments about honour and leadership. It changes markedly from the invading Horde of the early games.

Burning Legion

A demonic army that threatens multiple worlds. Warcraft III and Legion offer the clearest focused route into this conflict.

Dragonflights

Dragon communities with distinct responsibilities and histories. Dragonflight introduces their present-day concerns.

Void

A cosmic force whose influence matters increasingly in the Worldsoul Saga. Alleria and Xal’atath provide two very different points of contact.
