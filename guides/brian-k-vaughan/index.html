Brian K. Vaughan: Where to Start and What to Read Next

By Peter D’Souza
BRIAN K. VAUGHAN
Fourth Frame
Guide / 6 min

*Start with Paper Girls if you want a complete original series, Saga Vol. 1 if you want to join an unfinished epic, or Doctor Strange: The Oath for a short complete superhero book. For a longer finished journey, choose Y: The Last Man*.

These books occupy separate continuities. A shared writer does not make one a prequel to another. The routes below distinguish completed stories, author handovers and collection overlap, without giving away the major endings. Reviewed on 17 October 2026.

For the critical discussion, read The Best Brian K. Vaughan Comics. Compare writers through our five-author starting list.

Choose a commitment

  • One compact superhero story: Doctor Strange: The Oath #1–5.
  • A complete original mystery: The Private Eye, ten parts.
  • A complete medium-length series: Paper Girls #1–30.
  • A complete long series: Y: The Last Man #1–60.
  • An unfinished family space opera: Saga, beginning at #1.
  • Teenage Marvel adventure: Vaughan's Runaways, beginning with the 2003 series.
  • Politics and a superhero mystery: the completed Ex Machina.

Saga: know what the large collections contain

Begin with Saga Vol. 1, collecting #1–6, and continue in numbered trade order. The series requires no earlier comics. Its explicit sexual material, nudity, language and violence make it adult reading, despite the family at its centre.1

Saga Compendium One gathers #1–54, equivalent to the original standard trades Volumes 1–9. The first three deluxe hardcovers divide the same fifty-four issues into eighteen-issue books. Deluxe Book Four contains #55–72, equivalent to the next three six-issue trades. These are overlapping formats, not separate phases that all need to be purchased.2

The series is not complete at this guide's review date. “Compendium One” is a large opening portion, not a full-story guarantee. If owning a complete ending is essential to the purchase, choose Paper Girls or Y instead. No final issue number or future release schedule is assumed here.

Reading an entire collected arc before looking at the next volume's blurb helps preserve the surprises. Retail descriptions frequently mention developments that the preceding book has not yet revealed.

Paper Girls: thirty issues, one complete story

Read #1–30 in order. Choose six standard trades, three deluxe hardcovers, or Paper Girls: The Complete Story, which gathers the entire run. Newer smaller-format editions divide the material differently, so check their issue lists before mixing them with older volumes.3

The cast are twelve-year-olds, but the book includes violence, strong language and period slurs. It is a teen-oriented time-travel adventure rather than a title for small children. Cliff Chiang and Matt Wilson's visual storytelling is a major attraction; inspect reproduction size and legibility when choosing a compact edition.

The screen adaptation is separate. You do not need to watch it before reading, and its continuation status does not determine whether the comic has an ending.

Y: The Last Man: a full journey with a final chapter

Read #1–60. Available complete routes include the original ten standard trades, five deluxe books, or the omnibus. Compendium formats offer another route, but confirm the ranges within the particular set rather than assuming one volume is enough. Unmanned is the opening standard trade.4

The book's sex-and-gender premise reflects its early-2000s context and has limitations. It also contains strong language, sexual material and violence. Readers can appreciate its relationships while disagreeing with aspects of its framing.

You can stop at #60 knowing you have reached the series finale. Avoid online biographies while reading: a single sentence about a companion can give away years of narrative development. Pia Guerra and José Marzán Jr. are central to the project's human continuity, with other artists contributing along the way.

Runaways: separate Vaughan from later creators

The Vaughan route is Runaways (2003) #1–18 → Runaways (2005) #1–24. The second series resets the numbering; it is a continuation rather than an alternative version of the first eighteen issues. Vaughan's departure after the second series' #24 is an author boundary, not the permanent end of the characters' adventures.

The opening Runaways: The Complete Collection with the 2003 #1–18 is a useful first purchase and a meaningful initial stopping point. Collections that cover the full Vaughan run span more material. Do not assume the word “Complete” on one opening volume means every issue he wrote.5

Adrian Alphona is the defining artistic collaborator, with Takeshi Miyazawa and others contributing. Joss Whedon's subsequent arc and Rainbow Rowell's later revival are different author projects. They are optional continuations after you finish the selected Vaughan route.

No Avengers or X-Men prerequisite is needed. Let the children discover their own circumstances before reading summaries of their parents.

The Private Eye, The Oath and Pride of Baghdad

The Private Eye: read the ten-part story from the beginning. The original digital route is through Panel Syndicate; Image offers collected print formats. Marcos Martín's landscape compositions make a tablet or suitably sized print edition especially comfortable. Do not buy the print book as though it were a sequel to the digital story; it collects that work.6

Doctor Strange: The Oath: read #1–5, or the single collected volume. It is independent of the current Doctor Strange run and the films. Vaughan and Martín make it approachable without a previous Strange reading project. Look for the exact subtitle; a collection of unrelated Doctor Strange stories is not required.7

Pride of Baghdad: a standalone graphic novel with Niko Henrichon. One complete edition is enough. The animal protagonists do not make its war story gentle or suitable for young children. It is an optional branch, not a prelude to the author's larger series.8

Ex Machina: the political branch

Begin Ex Machina #1 and follow the main series through #50, with Special #1–4 in the placement supplied by your collected edition. The Complete Series Omnibus includes that full range; smaller numbered book sequences divide it across several volumes.9

Tony Harris is the principal artist. The book combines the responsibilities of New York's mayor with a mystery around the protagonist's former superhero career. Its politics belong to its post-9/11 American setting, so approach the debates in that context. It is unrelated to the film with the same title.

The simplest first shelf

For finished stories, try The Oath → Paper Girls → Y: The Last Man. That moves from five issues to thirty to sixty without imposing any narrative dependency. For the shortest original route, substitute The Private Eye for the superhero opening.

If Saga is what actually interests you, begin there. The point of distinguishing completion is to make the choice clear, not to make an unfinished work off-limits. Buy one opening volume before committing to a large overlapping format. No current local prices or stock are assumed in this guide.
