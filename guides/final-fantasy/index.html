Games / Final Fantasy

Choose a world. You do not need the whole series.

Final Fantasy is a family of worlds, not one story running from I to XVI. Familiar creatures, magic and names invite recognition, while most numbered games introduce new people and histories. Pick the kind of journey you want, then follow that world’s relationships and conflicts.

Our starting recommendation: Start X for a clear, turn-based journey into an unfamiliar culture, IX for storybook fantasy with thoughtful characters, or XVI for action and a darker political world. VI is an excellent pixel-era choice. VII Remake begins a connected modern project; it is not a replacement that contains the entire 1997 story.

Choose your way in.

I want turn-based combat and a clear story

Final Fantasy X

Tidus enters Spira as an outsider, so the world explains itself alongside him. The pilgrimage gives you a focused route through people, places and beliefs.

Know this much
Sin threatens Spira. Yuna is a summoner undertaking a pilgrimage, and Tidus becomes one of her companions.
You can skip
I–IX, every optional boss, and X-2 until you want to stay in this world.
The trade-off
Its linear journey and deliberate battles differ from open-world RPGs. Some optional challenges are far more demanding than the main story.
Go next
Choose X-2 only after X if you want more of Yuna and Spira. It changes tone and combat; you can finish X and stop.

I want warm fantasy with serious questions

Final Fantasy IX

A theatre troupe, a princess and a young mage offer an inviting opening. Their worries about identity, belonging and duty grow out of the adventure rather than a lore lecture.

Know this much
Zidane’s troupe visits Alexandria, where Garnet’s concerns and Vivi’s unexpected involvement bring them together.
You can skip
Every other Final Fantasy, a glossary of recurring monsters, and optional completion challenges.
The trade-off
Its older battle pace and presentation may require adjustment. The playful look does not mean every part of the story is lighthearted.
Go next
VI offers another ensemble story about power and identity; X offers a more directed pilgrimage. Neither is a continuation you must play.

I want a modern cast and action with tactical choices

Final Fantasy VII Remake

Midgar gives Cloud’s party a strong local beginning. Their competing motives make the conflict with Shinra understandable before the larger mysteries widen.

Know this much
Cloud accepts work with Avalanche, a group opposing a company that draws energy from the planet.
You can skip
I–VI, the 1997 game before beginning, Crisis Core as compulsory homework, and the films or mobile games.
The trade-off
This is the first part of a connected reimagining, not the entire original VII. Familiar material develops differently; first-time players may find some late mysteries deliberately unresolved.
Go next
Continue Rebirth. Intergrade is an expanded edition of Remake, and its INTERmission episode is an optional Yuffie detour before Rebirth. The project’s final announced chapter is future orientation here. Choose original VII instead if you want the complete original story in one game.

I want the pixel-era ensemble and its lore

Final Fantasy VI, including Pixel Remaster

Different people reveal different costs of imperial power. A relatively compact visual style makes its towns, character scenes and escalating conflict easy to read.

Know this much
An empire turns rediscovered magic into military power. Terra’s place in that system is the opening question.
You can skip
I–V, buying the whole Pixel Remaster collection, and finding every secret before advancing.
The trade-off
It uses older navigation and timed command battles. There is no single protagonist whose route substitutes for the whole ensemble.
Go next
Try IV for a more focused redemption story, or IX for another cast wrestling with belonging. Each begins afresh.

I want action combat and a darker political story

Final Fantasy XVI

Clive’s family bonds and the treatment of people with magic give its wars concrete human stakes. The game introduces its own vocabulary and nations.

Know this much
Valisthea’s nations depend on powerful crystals. Certain people wield Eikons, while others are exploited for magical ability.
You can skip
All earlier Final Fantasy games, understanding every summon before playing, and the expansions before the base adventure.
The trade-off
It focuses on one directly controlled fighter rather than turn-based party management. Violence and adult themes make it a different tone from IX.
Go next
Echoes of the Fallen and The Rising Tide are optional additions with late-game requirements. XV offers a separate action-oriented road trip with a stronger traveling-party emphasis.

I want a long story in an online world

Final Fantasy XIV: A Realm Reborn

Your own adventurer grows into a central role while recurring allies give an expanding world continuity. Start with the main scenario rather than treating years of updates as an immediate obligation.

Know this much
Begin as an adventurer in Eorzea. Main Scenario Quests introduce the institutions, companions and conflicts you need.
You can skip
I–XIII, the retired original XIV release, XI, raids and other optional activities until they interest you.
The trade-off
It is a substantial online commitment with multiplayer elements. Account, trial and subscription conditions vary; check the official options before choosing an edition.
Go next
Continue the main scenario through Heavensward → Stormblood → Shadowbringers → Endwalker → Dawntrail as your interest grows. Finishing a chapter is a reasonable stopping point; the guide does not require catching up to every patch.

Enough to begin.

The number usually marks a new world

X is not a sequel to IX. A suffix often signals a direct continuation: X-2 follows X, and XIII-2 follows XIII. The VII remake project is a connected sequence within VII’s world. Those local connections matter more than the franchise number.

Recognizable ideas, different lore

Crystals, summons, chocobos, moogles and people named Cid recur. Their identities and functions change by game. A Cid in one world does not normally carry another Cid’s biography, and a familiar summon need not have the same origin.

Choose a play style as well as a story

Combat ranges from command-based parties to real-time action and online role-playing. XI and XIV are separate online games; neither requires the other. Early North American releases called IV “II” and VI “III”; this guide uses the original Japanese numbering to avoid that confusion.

The story, at your pace.

Titles and starting premises are visible. Story outcomes stay hidden until you choose to reveal them. You can play a recommended route without completing this timeline.

Release years use first original releases, usually in Japan; editions may have later dates. “Group connected stories” places each world together and orders its included connections. It does not create a chronology from I to XVI. Crisis Core is earlier within original VII’s world but is recommended later for first-time mystery preservation. The remake project has a separate group and spoiler boundary. The starting-route filter shows alternative beginnings and Remake’s direct released follow-up, not a compulsory sequence across worlds.

The bigger picture.

VII: Shinra, Avalanche and the planet

Shinra’s energy monopoly connects comfort and prosperity to extracting the planet’s life. Avalanche resists it, but individual members’ motives differ. That tension explains the opening without needing the whole Compilation of VII.

X: belief, Sin and the pilgrimage

Yevon’s teachings shape how Spira understands recurring disaster. Yuna’s guardians and the people they meet reveal what that belief costs and what it sustains. Let the pilgrimage disclose the deeper truths rather than beginning with an ending summary.

VI: empire, Returners and espers

The empire seeks to turn magical beings and people into instruments of conquest. The resistance gathers different lives affected by that project. Terra and Celes show that being made useful to power is not the same as choosing a purpose.

XVI: crystals, Bearers and Dominants

Mothercrystals underpin national power. Bearers are exploited for magic, while Dominants’ Eikons make them strategic assets. The distinctions matter because the same society assigns radically different value to different people’s abilities.

Ivalice: politics without compulsory crossover

XII and Tactics each make war, institutions and unequal status central to their own casts. Shared names and setting associations enrich comparison; this guide gives each a separate route and makes no claim that one is required background for the other.

XI and XIV: separate online worlds

Vana’diel and Eorzea are different commitments. Begin the chosen game’s introductory missions or main scenario, then follow its own expansions. This guide introduces the worlds rather than summarizing years of online story outcomes.
