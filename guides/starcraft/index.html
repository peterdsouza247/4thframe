Games / StarCraft

Pick a campaign. Learn the war through its people.

StarCraft’s story is a chain of betrayals, loyalties, and competing perspectives. The campaigns teach their armies, so you can begin without multiplayer expertise.

Our starting recommendation: Start Wings of Liberty for the most accessible campaign introduction. Choose StarCraft and Brood War, through Remastered, if you want Kerrigan and Raynor’s full original story.

I want the easiest campaign introduction

StarCraft II: Wings of Liberty

Raynor’s ship, crew, and mission choices give the conflict a human centre. Tutorials teach the army as the story grows.

The starting situation contains major original-story developments. Read the short recap if you accept those spoilers.

Continue to Heart of the Swarm, then Whispers of Oblivion and Legacy of the Void.

I want the original betrayals and relationships

StarCraft / Remastered

The terran, zerg, and protoss campaigns establish the people whose choices drive both games. Remastered includes the original and Brood War stories.

Know this much
The sector’s human government faces rebellion and alien threats.
You can skip
StarCraft II, books, competitive build orders, and perfect mission scores.
The trade-off
Older pathfinding and controls require patience. Play the campaign episodes in their intended sequence.
Go next
Continue directly to Brood War, then Wings of Liberty.

I want to lead the protoss

Legacy of the Void

Artanis’s coalition has a clear immediate purpose, and its army offers a different style of strategy.

Know this much
The protoss seek to reclaim their home while a larger threat manipulates the conflict.
You can skip
A full three-army marathon if the protoss are what brought you here.
The trade-off
This is the saga’s concluding campaign, so its recap and epilogue reveal much of the earlier story.
Go next
Play Whispers of Oblivion first; use earlier recaps if desired, then the main campaign and epilogue.

Enough to begin.

The Koprulu sector

Human colonies, the zerg, and the protoss fight over a region far from Earth.

Three perspectives

Terrans, zerg, and protoss have different institutions and motives. Their campaigns are sequential viewpoints on a shared conflict.

Campaign difficulty

A comfortable setting lets you learn strategy without ranked-play expectations. Challenge modes and co-op commanders are optional.

The bigger picture.

Terran governments

The Confederacy, Dominion, UED, and rebels have different interests. “Terran” is a species grouping, not one faction.

The Swarm

Zerg leadership and purpose change across the campaigns; Kerrigan is not their only historical authority.

Protoss communities

Aiur’s templar and the dark templar carry old divisions; Artanis’s project seeks cooperation beyond them.

Xel’naga

Ancient creators and their legacy connect the local wars to the saga’s larger threat.
