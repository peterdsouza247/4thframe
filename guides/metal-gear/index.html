Games / Metal Gear

Choose a Snake. Keep the surprises intact.

Metal Gear combines military thrillers, family connections, and arguments about loyalty and control. Its prequels explain earlier history after the audience has already met its consequences, so chronological order is not automatically the best first route.

Our starting recommendation: Start Metal Gear Solid for the central Solid Snake story, then follow release order. Choose Metal Gear Solid 3 or its Delta remake for an accessible, largely self-contained Cold War prequel.

Choose your way in.

I want the central story and its surprises

Metal Gear Solid

Shadow Moses introduces Snake, his support team, and the series’ mix of stealth and political intrigue in one focused mission. Optional recaps cover the two MSX games.

Know this much
A veteran operative is sent to resolve a hostage crisis involving a nuclear threat.
You can skip
Playing the two older Metal Gear games, every codec conversation, and the prequels before starting.
The trade-off
Its controls and camera reflect its era. The older games add history, but the briefing and recap can provide enough context.
Go next
Continue Metal Gear Solid 2, then 3, then 4. Follow with Peace Walker, Ground Zeroes, and The Phantom Pain if Big Boss’s story interests you.

I want a focused espionage prequel

Metal Gear Solid 3 / Delta: Snake Eater

A Cold War mission introduces its own cast and emotional conflict. Delta retells this campaign with newer presentation.

Know this much
Naked Snake is operating in 1964 amid a crisis involving the Soviet Union, a weapon project, and his mentor.
You can skip
The earlier released games before this adventure.
The trade-off
Starting with the prequel changes how later connections land. Avoid online character biographies, which often reveal their entire future.
Go next
Return to Metal Gear Solid and continue release order. Alternatively follow the Big Boss-era games after accepting that you are seeing history before its later reveals.

I mainly want open-ended stealth missions

The Phantom Pain

Its flexible bases and tools make it a strong gameplay-first entry, even when the wider conspiracy is not your immediate interest.

Know this much
An independent military organisation has been devastated; its survivors seek to rebuild and pursue those responsible.
You can skip
A complete saga marathon and optional collectible recordings.
The trade-off
Its narrative depends on Peace Walker and Ground Zeroes, and important context sits outside the missions. It is a weaker first choice if story clarity matters most.
Go next
Read the optional bridge recap, then play missions at your own pace. Try Snake Eater or Metal Gear Solid afterwards for a more concentrated narrative.

Enough to begin.

A recurring weapon idea

Metal Gear is the name given to weapon systems tied to nuclear deterrence and military power. Each crisis asks who controls that power.

More than one Snake

Solid Snake and the young soldier called Naked Snake are different protagonists in different eras. The guide keeps later identity revelations inside recaps.

Release order versus history

Prequels were written after later-set games. Starting with one is valid, but a historical sort should not be mistaken for a spoiler-free viewing plan.

The bigger picture.

Military organisations

FOX, FOXHOUND, MSF, and Diamond Dogs belong to different periods and command structures. Similar names do not make them interchangeable.

Cipher and the Patriots

These names belong to the saga’s struggle over control. Their deeper relationships are part of the story’s reveals, not prerequisites for starting.

Private military forces

Later games ask how war changes when organisations and technologies profit from keeping it active.

The support team

Radio calls provide character and practical context. Listen when interested; memorising every acronym is unnecessary.
