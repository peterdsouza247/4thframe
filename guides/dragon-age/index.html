Games / Dragon Age

Choose your chapter of Thedas.

Each major Dragon Age game introduces a new protagonist. Start with the style of adventure you want, then learn the older decisions that matter to its people.

Our starting recommendation: Choose Origins for the richest introduction to Thedas’s institutions, Inquisition for a broad modern adventure, or The Veilguard for a more direct action RPG. None requires a full series marathon.

Choose your way in.

I want the political and cultural foundation

Dragon Age: Origins

The playable origins introduce how class, ancestry, faith, and power shape ordinary lives before the wider crisis.

Know this much
Grey Wardens fight darkspawn; a Blight threatens Ferelden.
You can skip
All other games, novels, and downloadable side stories.
The trade-off
Its tactical combat and presentation are older. Pause often and choose a difficulty that lets you enjoy the party.
Go next
Finish Origins, then Awakening if you want more Wardens. Move to Dragon Age II when ready.

I want a large adventure with a new hero

Dragon Age: Inquisition

The opening crisis gives the new Inquisitor a clear role. The party gradually explains the region’s tensions.

Know this much
Mages and templars are in conflict; a breach has opened in the sky.
You can skip
Full Origins and DAII playthroughs, map completion, and multiplayer.
The trade-off
Some returning people will have more history than you. Avoid clearing every Hinterlands task before moving on.
Go next
Follow the main story and companions, then Trespasser as the epilogue. Other DLC is optional.

I want the most direct action RPG

Dragon Age: The Veilguard

Rook and a new party offer an immediate way into northern Thedas. The campaign supplies its urgent problem.

Know this much
Solas plans to alter the Veil; Varric’s team seeks to stop the consequences.
You can skip
A complete three-game playthrough, novels, and podcasts.
The trade-off
Inquisition and Trespasser give Solas’s story more personal weight. This game carries a narrower set of earlier choices.
Go next
Use the short Inquisition/Trespasser recap if you accept those spoilers, then prioritise the companion missions.

Enough to begin.

Thedas

A continent whose nations, faiths, and histories give different answers to the same conflicts.

The Fade and the Veil

The Fade is tied to dreams and spirits. The Veil separates it from the physical world; magic can make that boundary dangerous.

A changing world

Rulers, companion outcomes, and romances can vary. Later games preserve some earlier choices, but not every decision carries into every sequel.

The bigger picture.

Grey Wardens

An order defined by its fight against darkspawn and the sacrifices that duty demands.

Chantry, circles, and templars

Faith, magical confinement, and military enforcement generate conflicts that differ across nations.

Elves and Tevinter

Elven communities and Tevinter’s institutions hold sharply unequal power. Their histories should not be reduced to one shared viewpoint.

Veilguard allies

The Shadow Dragons, Antivan Crows, Veil Jumpers, Mourn Watch, and Lords of Fortune give Rook’s party different local stakes.
