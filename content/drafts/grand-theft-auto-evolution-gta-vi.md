*From stolen hatchbacks to criminal empires, how GTA changed its cities, its stories and its idea of freedom, and what GTA VI needs to get right.*

There is a moment in Grand Theft Auto when the mission stops mattering.

You were supposed to collect somebody. Then a better car went past. Then the radio played something you liked. Then the police arrived, an unfortunate misunderstanding involving several pedestrians having occurred, and now you are reversing a burning ambulance up a motorway exit.

Your contact can wait. He has been standing in the same place since Tuesday.

Almost thirty years into the series, that diversion remains GTA's most persuasive sales pitch. Rockstar can write the betrayal, hire the actor and choreograph the helicopter crash. It still has to compete with whatever happens when you take the wrong turn.

GTA VI is currently scheduled for **19 November 2026**, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. Rockstar's launch trailer arrived on 15 October. The return to Vice City is close enough to require actual calendar management, rather than another theory about the moon.[^1]

But what, exactly, are we waiting to return to?

For some players, GTA is the neon of Vice City. For others, it is a bicycle outside the Johnson house, a boat arriving in Liberty City, or three men arguing their way through a robbery. A growing part of its audience knows Los Santos primarily as somewhere to meet friends, run businesses and be murdered by equipment that appears to have escaped from a defence contractor's cocaine drawer.

These games share a name and a remarkably durable appetite for stealing cars. Their ambitions have changed considerably. They have offered the fantasy of becoming powerful, asked whether power fixes anything, and eventually built an online economy that can make criminal prosperity feel suspiciously like maintaining a second job.

To understand what VI might become, it helps to follow how that happened. The story starts in Scotland, well before anyone needed to count the hair on a trailer character's arm.

*Spoiler note: this article discusses premises, themes and several mid-story developments across the released games. It avoids explaining their final choices and endings. GTA VI discussion uses public material available by 16 October 2026.*

## 1. Before the empire: GTA discovers that the detour is the game

### Grand Theft Auto, 1997

The original GTA arrived in a period when games were enthusiastically learning to look expensive. PlayStation was selling the promise of three-dimensional worlds; here was a top-down game in which your criminal ambitions were represented by a small person you could lose beneath the scenery.

DMA Design's project had begun as **Race 'n' Chase**. Its development was messy, and the eventual formula emerged through iteration rather than a single immaculate design revelation. In a later interview, developer Gary Penn recalled instability, awful vehicle handling and a police-behaviour bug that made officers aggressively ram the player. He remembered that aggression helping the game become entertaining. It is a valuable recollection, with the uncertainty he attached to it intact: one developer's account of a breakthrough, rather than proof that the entire franchise happened by accident.[^2]

The result let you move between vehicles, crimes and jobs across early versions of **Liberty City, San Andreas and Vice City**. Those names preceded their famous later incarnations. Narrative arrived in small messages and instructions; the real continuity was the money you earned and the trouble you accumulated.[^3]

That abstraction mattered. Early GTA treated the city as something to interfere with. Missions gave you reasons to travel, but stealing a different vehicle could change the texture of the next minute. You could understand the place through what it allowed, and through the speed at which it became annoyed with you.

It also established the radio. Much of the first game's music was created in-house by Colin Anderson, Craig Conner and Grant Middleton, rather than assembled from the licensed hits that would later become a series trademark.[^4] Even here, a car was both transport and a little room with music in it. GTA had found a way to make travelling entertaining before it could afford to make travelling beautiful.

Its humour was less discriminating. The notorious bonus for running down a procession of Hare Krishnas belongs to this history too. Some of the early transgression is clever interaction; some is simply a game noticing that it can be offensive and getting rather pleased with itself. Those impulses would remain difficult to separate.

The surrounding panic arrived astonishingly early. In May 1997, before the game's release, GTA was being discussed in the House of Lords. Lord Campbell of Croy raised concerns about a game involving theft and police pursuits.[^5] The institution was taking the threat seriously while the developers were still trying to make the cars behave.

That mismatch became part of GTA's public identity. Adults arguing that a game was dangerous gave it an aura no advertising department could buy quite as convincingly. Yet outrage alone does not explain its endurance. Plenty of offensive games have disappeared without requiring an international countdown for their sequels. GTA had a repeatable pleasure beneath the scandal: a small decision could produce a large mess.

### London 1969 and London 1961, 1999

The two London mission packs are often treated as a geographical footnote. They deserve better. They showed, early, that changing a GTA's time and place could change its personality even when its underlying actions stayed familiar.

**London 1969** moved the formula into a British period-crime setting: left-hand traffic, different slang, vehicles and a soundtrack that helped establish the era. The smaller **London 1961** followed as a free PC expansion.[^6]

This was the seed of an approach Vice City would make famous. A setting could determine the jokes, the musical references and the sort of criminal fantasy being offered. You could steal a car in several games; the question was what stealing that car made you feel you were starring in.

The London packs also complicate the claim that GTA has always been exclusively about America. It became overwhelmingly associated with America. For a brief period it was perfectly happy to send you down a British street, where organised crime could be conducted in the vicinity of a reasonably priced sandwich.

### GTA 2, 1999: somebody remembers who you annoyed

GTA 2 kept the overhead view but moved into the deliberately strange, retrofuturistic **Anywhere City**. It also made gang relationships central. Work for one faction, damage another, and watch your standing change. Hostile territory could become a practical problem rather than a different colour on the map.[^7]

Here was an early attempt to give freedom consequences beyond the police chase. The city had competing interests. Your access to work depended on how you positioned yourself among them.

Its premise remained broader and less intimate than the character dramas to come. Claude Speed was not asking players to spend fifty hours processing the emotional history of a tracksuit. The gangs were extravagant types, with the Zaibatsu corporation making the overlap between criminal and corporate power especially unsubtle.

At launch, however, refinement could look like repetition. GameSpot's contemporary review enjoyed the game while stressing how much of it remained familiar.[^7] That is useful perspective. GTA was not born as an unstoppable sequence of unanimously recognised masterpieces. The early games were finding their limits, too.

One of their most interesting ideas would later become oddly peripheral: working relationships that change the way the city receives you. GTA VI could learn as much from that respect meter as from another decade of visual improvements.

## 2. GTA III: suddenly, the city has a horizon

**GTA III arrived on PlayStation 2 in October 2001.** Putting the series into 3D changed the player's relationship with almost everything.

The road now ran away from you. A building could conceal an escape route. A hill could reveal the next district. Driving towards a bridge felt different from moving a small car towards a line on a map. You occupied the city from inside the traffic.

The breakthrough was the combination: driving, on-foot movement, gunfights, ambient pedestrians, police escalation and a crime story all operating in one explorable place. GTA III did not invent open worlds. It made this particular mixture feel like something an enormous number of people had been waiting to play. Its launch reviews recognised that immediately, even while acknowledging awkward controls and other rough edges.[^8]

Claude, betrayed during a robbery, works through Liberty City's criminal networks. He barely needs a personality for the structure to function. Other people supply the threats, plans and self-importance; he supplies the labour. It is an arrangement familiar to anyone who has attended a meeting where somebody says, “We should really own this.”

The mob-film influences gave the game an accessible dramatic language. You knew roughly what kind of room you were entering when a crime boss called. The city supplied the atmosphere: industrial grime, rain, commercial clutter and an impression that almost everybody was making a living at somebody else's expense.

Its side activities helped loosen the boundaries of the fantasy. A taxi, ambulance or police vehicle could become the starting point for another sort of play. You might be the least trustworthy emergency worker in the borough, but the city still had a vacancy.

Then there was **Chatterbox**. Radio turned the journeys between missions into encounters with people you never met. Commercials, callers and presenters suggested a society that continued being ridiculous while Claude was elsewhere. It was world-building that did not require the player to stop, read a document and demonstrate sufficient interest in the lore.

GTA III's release environment also demands care. It launched weeks after the September 11 attacks, with Rockstar's New York operation directly affected. Later, the studio explained that it made limited changes, including alterations to packaging and some content. Its own retrospective pushed back against the mythology of a wholesale, last-minute rebuilding of the game.[^9]

The historical distinction matters. A game developed before the attacks entered a profoundly different public atmosphere after them. That does not make every bridge, damaged car or police encounter an intentional response to the event.

GTA III nevertheless established the series' defining tension. Liberty City appeared open to improvisation, while its story required a particular sequence of jobs. The player could discover a hundred ways to make trouble between missions, then encounter a mission that preferred one approved kind of trouble.

For the moment, the city was extraordinary enough to make that bargain easy.

## 3. Vice City: ambition gets a swimming pool

One year later, **Vice City** returned with an argument for style.

Its 1986 setting brought pastel suits, beachside hotels, cocaine fortunes and the combined gravitational pull of *Scarface* and *Miami Vice*. Tommy Vercetti, voiced by Ray Liotta, arrives after a prison sentence and immediately loses his employer's money in a drug deal gone wrong. He spends the game recovering his position, discovering the city's opportunities and becoming considerably more difficult to manage.[^10]

Giving the lead a voice changed the transaction. Claude received everybody else's personality. Tommy pushed back. He could be impatient, sarcastic and furious, allowing a scene to become a contest rather than a briefing delivered to a leather jacket.

The property system gave his ascent a physical shape. Businesses generated work and eventually income. The city could gradually become a collection of things you owned.[^10] Buying a building was satisfying because it made the fantasy legible: you had started as somebody being shouted at over a telephone, and now other people were doing the worrying.

This was GTA's American dream at its most seductive. Yes, the route involved murder. But look at the house.

Vice City's critique of greed and its pleasure in acquisition continually fed each other. The game mocked status while furnishing an excellent selection of status symbols. It understood the attraction of the thing it was ridiculing, which helped the satire avoid the deadness of a lecture written by somebody who had never wanted anything expensive.

The music performed a similar trick. Pop, rock, new wave, electro, soul and talk radio made the period feel coherent without making every inhabitant listen to the same thing. The game could sell a fantasy of the eighties and make fun of the people selling that fantasy, sometimes within the same drive.[^11]

Vice City was released in 2002, looking back at 1986. The distance was sixteen years. GTA VI is arriving twenty-four years after Vice City, with more than twice as much distance from 1986 as the original game had.

Nostalgia has had time to develop nostalgia.

That arithmetic matters because Vice City's atmosphere now comes to players through two memories: the decade it depicted, and the period when they first played it. A modern Vice City cannot reproduce that by adding pink lights. Some of the glow belongs to an old television, a particular room and the absence of adult responsibilities.

The game had limits. Its ethnic gang caricatures can make its supposedly expansive city feel small-minded. Its treatment of women often reduces them to scenery, temptation or a joke. Calling the setting satirical does not settle whether an individual gag has observed anything worth laughing at.

Even so, Vice City made a lasting discovery. A GTA city could become memorable because its parts belonged together. Its strongest identity came from the relationship between the light, the music, the vehicles and Tommy's appetite for ownership. More activities would have meant little if they had broken that mood.

## 4. San Andreas: home becomes an entire state

If Vice City was a beautifully dressed argument for excess, **San Andreas** brought a removal van.

Released in 2004 and set in 1992, it followed **Carl “CJ” Johnson** home to Los Santos after his mother's murder. Family obligations, a weakened neighbourhood gang and corrupt police established a more personal starting point than Tommy's missing investment. The game drew on the cultural world of films such as *Boyz n the Hood* and *Menace II Society*, while its Los Angeles references included gang conflict and the history surrounding the 1992 unrest.[^12]

CJ has a home that matters before the player begins buying anything. That gives his story a different emotional centre. Where Tommy wants to acquire a city, CJ has to decide what he owes the place he left.

The eventual journey through **Los Santos, San Fierro, Las Venturas and the spaces between them** expands into something much stranger than that premise initially suggests. Street drama gives way to rural escapades, elaborate criminal operations and increasingly outrageous machinery.[^13]

The tonal lurch can be wonderful. It can also make the opening's human stakes feel as though they have been packed away for later. San Andreas sometimes handles narrative escalation like a child explaining a dream: and then there was a casino, and then the military, and then you could fly.

It works as often as it does because CJ remains personable. His exasperation makes a useful companion to the player's own. He gives the enormous game somebody to come back to.

Mechanically, San Andreas chased an unusually broad idea of inhabiting a character. Food, exercise, body shape, clothing, haircuts, skills and relationships could affect the experience. Swimming and climbing expanded how you moved. Gang territory made parts of Los Santos contested space. There were bicycles, aircraft and long journeys into places that felt distant from the starting neighbourhood.[^14]

Some of these systems were richer in possibility than in practice. A stat can encourage attachment, or it can ask you to perform maintenance before you are allowed to enjoy yourself. CJ's body gave player choices a visible consequence, but being told to improve a capability could also reveal how quickly immersion becomes homework.

The geography had a deeper effect. Rural roads, forests and desert provided contrast. Distance gave arrival a sense of occasion. San Fierro felt like somewhere you had travelled to, with a different pace and shape, rather than another set of streets unlocked on the same commute.

The radio helped carry those changes. Radio Los Santos grounded the West Coast rap identity, while K-Rose, K-DST and Radio X broadened the musical America surrounding it. Country music could accompany a stretch of open road; alternative rock could change the mood of another journey. A large map became several overlapping cultural spaces.[^15]

San Andreas is therefore a useful rebuke to the idea that density always beats scale, or scale always beats density. Its expansiveness worked because the places differed. A hundred extra kilometres of interchangeable motorway would not have done the same job.

### The scandal that swallowed the conversation

The game launched near the end of 2004, amid arguments about what blockbuster games could depict and who was actually buying them. The following year's **Hot Coffee** controversy transformed those arguments.

The important detail is that the sexual material was present but disabled in the shipped software. The modification exposed it; it did not simply invent the content from nothing. The ESRB changed the rating to Adults Only, retailers pulled copies, and a revised Mature-rated edition followed. In 2006, the FTC announced a settlement over allegations that relevant content had not been disclosed properly.[^16]

The comic irony is obvious: an industry and political culture already familiar with the game's extensive violence became convulsed by a hidden sex minigame. But reducing the whole episode to American prudishness misses the substantive ratings issue. Parents and retailers were relying on a description of what the product contained. The dispute was also about the accuracy of that description.

It helped make San Andreas a cultural object people argued about without necessarily understanding what playing it involved. CJ's family, the state's musical variety and its odd experiments in character embodiment had to compete for attention with something most ordinary players had never seen.

GTA was becoming so notorious that its reputation could obscure the game itself.

## 5. The pocket GTAs tried more than they get credit for

The numbered entries dominate the history. The portable games deserve room because they tested which parts of GTA were essential when the hardware could not do everything.

### GTA Advance, 2004: the alternative route

Released in the same year as San Andreas, **GTA Advance** returned to an overhead view on Game Boy Advance. Digital Eclipse built an original Liberty City story around **Mike**, whose plans to leave crime with his associate Vinnie collapse into a revenge plot.[^17]

In a market being shown the scale of San Andreas, its compromises were easy to notice. The setting and mission patterns echoed GTA III, while the hardware narrowed how the city could be presented. It was a separate game, though, rather than a tiny port of the year's console release.

Its historical value is partly that coexistence. For a while, GTA could mean both a sprawling console production and a more modest interpretation designed for another machine. The brand had not yet become synonymous with a single development event capable of swallowing a decade.

### Liberty City Stories, 2005: the city fits in a bag

**Liberty City Stories** made the more immediately spectacular handheld argument: a recognisable 3D GTA city could run on PSP.

Set in 1998, it follows **Toni Cipriani** back into the Leone family's work. Liberty City is familiar enough to create recognition, but earlier enough in its history for streets, relationships and power to sit differently.[^18]

The story returns to mob obligation, career advancement and the difficulty of pleasing people whose preferred management technique is murder. Its compact mission rhythm suited portable play, even where the underlying design remained familiar. Radio preserved much of the sense of occupying a place rather than visiting a succession of levels.

Released in the year of Hot Coffee, it also made the separation between an adult game and an adult-sized machine impossible to maintain. GTA could now accompany you on public transport. Everyone else on the bus would have to supply their own moral crisis.

### Vice City Stories, 2006: empire-building has a family cost

Set in 1984, **Vice City Stories** centres on **Victor Vance**, whose attempt to support his family through military service goes disastrously wrong. His brother Lance helps connect this earlier story to Vice City's familiar world.[^19]

Victor is an interestingly uncomfortable fit for GTA. He objects to the criminal world while repeatedly doing what it demands. That tension can communicate entrapment; it can also make the character seem less coherent than the game wants him to be. Contemporary reviews noticed the problem. The later series would revisit it with much greater ambition in Niko Bellic.

Its criminal-business system is the more useful forgotten inheritance. Businesses could be established, developed and fought over. Acquisition was attached to a contested city, making empire-building more active than simply collecting revenue from a completed property.[^20]

The musical period-setting remained central, and Phil Collins appeared as himself. It is difficult to imagine a more specific measure of the series' cultural reach than a game about criminal entrepreneurship making room for an actual Phil Collins performance.[^21]

Stories also hints at the kind of future GTA could choose: revisit a strong location with another protagonist and a different social position. A new camera angle on a city can be more revealing than another island attached to it.

## 6. GTA IV: the American dream answers the door in its underwear

**GTA IV arrived in April 2008**, and its Liberty City offered a different sort of promise.

**Niko Bellic**, an Eastern European war veteran, comes to America carrying trauma and unfinished business. His cousin Roman has advertised an enviable new life. The reality involves an unglamorous apartment, a taxi business and debts. Roman has, in effect, been maintaining a highly selective social feed by telephone.[^22]

The joke has affection behind it. Roman's exaggerations conceal hope as well as embarrassment. Niko can be funny, loyal and frightening within the same conversation. These are people with enough warmth to make their circumstances matter.

That warmth distinguishes IV's disillusionment from a general declaration that everybody is awful. Liberty City's promise is hollow, but friendship is not automatically meaningless. The game leaves you something to lose.

Its reputation as the sombre GTA is justified, though incomplete. Brucie exists. A city containing Brucie cannot reasonably claim to have abolished farce.

### Fewer toys, more weight

Compared with San Andreas, IV removed or narrowed many activities and systems. There was less scope for body transformation and no equivalent journey across three cities and their countryside. The emphasis shifted towards a denser urban setting, physical detail, cover-based combat, a more involved phone and social life, and online multiplayer.[^23]

The cars felt heavier. Braking became an opinion you needed to express early. Bodies and vehicles reacted with a physicality that could produce either ugly comedy or an unexpectedly uncomfortable sense of impact.

Those choices served the setting. Niko's Liberty City resists you. Traffic is traffic; a corner requires attention; a gunfight can feel messy rather than merely stylish. The game asks you to spend more time inside an ordinary urban life, even when that life has an extraordinary ammunition budget.

Not every demand improved the experience. Friendships made the supporting cast more present, but the requests could become irritating. A relationship system is doing something peculiar when the player starts regarding an invitation to socialise as an attack on their remaining free time.

Then there is Niko's central contradiction. He wants distance from violence, yet the game repeatedly uses his ability to inflict it as the engine of progress. Some of that is deliberate: his skills keep returning him to the world he hopes to escape. Some is the old GTA mission structure straining under a more serious character.

Players can deepen the mismatch by treating free roam as an extended atrocity exhibition. The story cannot account for everything you do between scenes. Niko's dramatic life and the player's recreational behaviour sometimes inhabit neighbouring apartments and refuse to speak.

Still, IV's ambitions matter. It asked a blockbuster audience to inhabit someone for whom arriving in America did not mean becoming important. Its Liberty City could make opportunity feel physically near and socially inaccessible. You could drive past the life you wanted without getting any closer to it.

### A game released into 2008

It is tempting to make IV sound like a direct response to the financial crash. The dates require more restraint.

The US recession had begun in December 2007, according to the NBER's later dating. IV launched the following April, months before Lehman Brothers collapsed in September.[^24] A game of this size was not written in response to events that happened after it shipped.

Its timing nevertheless changed the resonance of its story. A city built around money, ambition and unequal chances arrived while the real economy was moving towards disaster. The gap between the sales pitch and lived experience was becoming difficult to ignore outside the game, too.

Meanwhile, GTA itself was prospering spectacularly. Take-Two reported roughly six million copies sold to consumers and more than $500 million in estimated retail sales in the first week.[^25] A story about the unreliability of American opportunity had become a demonstration of the entertainment industry's considerable opportunities.

The radio made Liberty City feel inhabited by different communities. Russian-language music, reggae, hip-hop, rock and ambient selections offered more than a generic New York mood. Choosing a station could change which city's inhabitants seemed nearest to you.[^26]

This is one reason later soundtrack changes matter. In 2018, Rockstar confirmed that expiring licences would remove music from IV and its episodes, with a substantial effect on Vladivostok FM.[^27] A patched game could preserve the streets while altering part of their cultural memory. The map remained. The drive was different.

## 7. The 2009 games found three more ways to inhabit Liberty City

### The Lost and Damned: belonging can be a trap

**Johnny Klebitz** approaches Liberty City through the Lost motorcycle club. His conflict with its returning leader, Billy Grey, gives **The Lost and Damned** a more focused concern than becoming rich: what happens when loyalty to a group becomes loyalty to its worst decisions?[^28]

Its motorcycles, gang presence and harder musical identity give the same urban space another rhythm. Riding with a club changes the image of arrival. Niko often looks like someone trying to enter a world; Johnny looks like someone struggling to preserve the world he already has.

The tragedy lies in how little romantic freedom the biker ideal finally offers. The open road can still lead back to the same obligations, grudges and destructive people. Owning a leather jacket does not make a dysfunctional organisation less dysfunctional.

### The Ballad of Gay Tony: loyalty with better lighting

**The Ballad of Gay Tony** moves through **Luis Lopez**, his partnership with nightclub owner Tony Prince, celebrity culture and the expensive lunacy of people such as Yusuf Amir. Parachutes, extravagant missions and nightclub life restore a degree of showmanship that some players missed in IV.[^29]

Tony's sexuality is part of the story's world, but his relationship with Luis gives the episode more to work with than a title designed to cause a conversation. Luis has to navigate affection, exasperation, business and danger. He is protecting a person whose capacity for self-sabotage makes the job unusually demanding.

The game can be loud and ridiculous while allowing that bond to matter. It supplies a particularly useful answer to the idea that GTA must choose between feeling something and blowing something up. The explosion improves when you care who is standing nearby.

Together, the episodes form one of Rockstar's best experiments. Incidents and characters intersect, giving the impression of a city containing several stories rather than waiting exclusively for one protagonist. The same location becomes poorer, richer, more intimate or more absurd depending on who takes you through it.[^30]

For a series now burdened by the expectation that each new entry should rebuild the industry, these shorter works remain an appealing precedent. A city can sustain more than one substantial story. The next project need not begin by moving the coastline.

### Chinatown Wars: the spreadsheet is contraband

**Chinatown Wars**, first released for Nintendo DS in 2009, made an apparently backward-looking camera into a fresh design choice.

**Huang Lee** arrives in Liberty City carrying a family sword, is ambushed and becomes entangled in Triad succession and betrayal. Comic-style scenes and touchscreen interactions establish a different presentation from IV's performances.[^31]

The crucial system is drug trading. Buying cheaply, following market opportunities and selling elsewhere give you reasons to cross the city that arise from its economy. Stock in your possession changes what you stand to lose when the police become interested.[^32]

It is a nasty little commercial simulation, and unusually effective GTA design. The money is attached to decisions. The journey has stakes before a character phones to announce them.

Its wanted system also rewards knocking pursuing police vehicles out of action at lower wanted levels, putting more of the escape into what you do during a chase. The device-specific interactions can be fussy, but the game repeatedly tries to make the player participate rather than watch.[^33]

The release context was revealing. The DS's huge audience and GTA's huge name did not guarantee an immediate sales triumph. Contemporary reports described a disappointing US opening, and Nintendo later publicly discussed its frustration with the game's commercial performance.[^34]

That should not be inflated into a definitive explanation of lifetime sales, or evidence that adult games could never work on Nintendo hardware. It does show that acclaim, brand recognition and platform reach are different things. Chinatown Wars remains valuable because its solutions were specific to its format. Bigger GTA was not its only ambition.

## 8. GTA V: three men, one very expensive identity crisis

**GTA V launched on 17 September 2013**, at the end of the PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 generation. It went on to pass $1 billion in retail sales within three days.[^35]

By then the franchise was a major entertainment event before anybody had played the new release. GTA III had surprised people. V had to satisfy a world that expected to be astonished on schedule.

Its solution was partly structural: **Michael, Franklin and Trevor** offered three positions within the same Southern Californian economy.

Michael has the house, pool and money. He remains unhappy. Franklin wants a route out of small-time work and a neighbourhood future that seems already mapped out. Trevor operates with a mixture of grievance, cruelty and freedom that makes him both magnetic and exhausting. Switching between them changes geography, class position and temperament, sometimes in the middle of a mission.[^36]

Michael is especially useful to the series' argument. Vice City lets you earn the mansion. V asks you to visit the man inside it. He has acquired the lifestyle and now needs an audience for his dissatisfaction.

Franklin gives the story its clearest route of ascent, but too often becomes the reasonable person standing between more aggressively written personalities. Trevor brings the chaos some players expect from GTA into the authored story itself. His volatility closes part of the gap between a supposedly reluctant protagonist and a player who wants to turn a junction into an insurance event.

It creates other problems. Once atrocity is a regular means of establishing character, escalation can exhaust itself. Shock eventually needs something underneath it. Otherwise the game is just pressing harder on a doorbell everybody has stopped answering.

### The heist becomes the organising fantasy

V's heists give its criminal careers a compelling shared purpose. Planning, selecting approaches and moving between characters make a robbery feel like a coordinated production. The player gets several jobs in the same action film.[^36]

The lighter, more accessible driving and the return of a broad city-and-countryside environment support a sunnier, more elastic game than IV. Los Santos and Blaine County can accommodate an urban commute, a desert disaster and a scenic excursion without asking them to feel like the same experience.

The limitation is how much apparent planning remains under authorial control. You choose within a designed structure. The city outside offers improvisation; the spectacular mission often requires its machinery to run in a particular order. The better the production becomes, the more conspicuous its barriers can feel.

### America discovers it can film itself

V also arrived at a useful cultural hinge. Pew found that a majority of American adults owned smartphones in 2013: 56 per cent.[^37] Self-presentation, social platforms and access to the internet were becoming ordinary features of daily life on a much wider scale.

V's technology companies, entertainment culture, paparazzi and personal brands belong to that environment. Its Los Santos wants to be seen. Even failure benefits from suitable coverage.

The game is often sharpest about people who convert insecurity into products or power. It is less effective when every person enters a scene already carrying the entire joke about them. Constant contempt can flatten a society until its absurdity stops being surprising.

Its women suffer especially from that narrowness. Too many are written mainly to irritate, expose a man's frustrations or decorate an environment. Carolyn Petit's contemporary GameSpot review praised the game's achievements while criticising its misogyny and confused politics.[^38] These objections were present at launch; they have not been retroactively invented by people who arrived too late to understand the fun.

There is also the torture mission. The scene can be read as attacking the usefulness and moral evasions of state violence, but the game makes the player perform the violence before delivering its critique. That is a deliberate, troubling construction. It is fair to ask whether the disgust serves the argument, or whether the argument is being asked to excuse another exercise in provocation.[^39]

### The music learns to follow the mission

V's score added another layer to the series' musical identity. **Tangerine Dream, Woody Jackson, The Alchemist and Oh No** contributed to the original score, with DJ Shadow involved in shaping its soundtrack presentation.[^40]

Radio could keep defining the world around you while composed music shaped the immediate action. A chase now had the city in one ear and a thriller gathering momentum in the other.

That expansion makes music's importance even clearer. GTA is unusually good at giving players memories that are half action and half song. You may forget which mission paid for a car. You remember what was playing when you drove it into the sea.

## 9. GTA Online: the empire becomes your employer

**GTA Online launched on 1 October 2013**, two weeks after V. Its opening was beset by connection and progression problems, with Rockstar publishing updates as it tried to stabilise the service.[^41]

That matters to the retrospective. Online did not arrive fully formed as the indestructible money-making institution later history can make it resemble. It grew through additions and revisions. The original cooperative Heists update arrived in March 2015; Cayo Perico followed in December 2020 and allowed its heist to be tackled solo as well as with others.[^42]

Over time, Los Santos became a place to accumulate businesses, vehicles, properties, routines and shared memories. The audience was creating continuity that did not depend on a new authored protagonist.

This fulfilled a long-standing GTA possibility. The city could be a social setting. Friends could turn a well-designed operation into an appalling rescue attempt, with the resulting failure more memorable than a clean completion.

The cost was a different relationship with money.

In a conventional GTA campaign, acquisition tends to move you towards an eventual state of abundance. In Online, a continuing catalogue of desirable things gives abundance somewhere else to go. Businesses, requirements, payouts and purchasable GTA$ can make the pursuit of freedom feel like managing an expanding portfolio of obligations.[^43]

The joke writes itself: the series spent years mocking American consumerism, then offered a persistent world in which your criminal ambitions could be accelerated at checkout.

That does not make every transaction exploitative or every session a chore. Online has supplied enormous pleasure and sustained relationships. It does make monetisation part of the experience's meaning. When a game jokes about greed, the way it sells you things belongs in the conversation.

### Why people wanted to play ordinary jobs inside GTA

Roleplay communities revealed another appetite. A player might want to become a paramedic, mechanic, officer or small-time operator and maintain relationships inside a city, rather than pursue the next increase in destructive capability.

In August 2023, Cfx.re, the team behind FiveM and RedM, officially joined Rockstar.[^44] That establishes a real relationship between the company and major roleplay infrastructure. It does not, by itself, confirm how VI's multiplayer will operate.

The lesson is still useful. People can want *more life* in a GTA city without wanting more ammunition. A reliable neighbourhood, a small business or a reputation can give an ordinary street enormous significance.

The series began by letting you disrupt a city. Some players have spent years finding ways to belong to one.

## 10. The games changed. So did the company selling them

A retrospective that stops at the software risks making GTA seem like an unbroken stream of ideas from an unchanged group of people.

It is a large, collaborative production, and its personnel have changed. Dan Houser left Rockstar in 2020. Leslie Benzies and Lazlow are other prominent figures from earlier GTA eras who departed.[^45] Recognising their contributions is reasonable. Declaring that a vast studio's future can be calculated from one departure is considerably less so.

The work between GTA V and VI also included **Red Dead Redemption 2**. Thirteen years between numbered GTA releases does not mean thirteen years spent solely on the same game. Nor can the gap simply be explained as Rockstar discovering that Online made money and locking the writers in a cupboard.

Success does, however, change the pressure on a new release. A franchise expected to sustain a large audience for years faces different incentives from one turning out a strange handheld experiment. Financial scale can buy extraordinary craftsmanship while making risk more expensive to approve.

Preserving the earlier games has exposed another problem. **The Trilogy: The Definitive Edition** launched in November 2021 with technical and presentation issues serious enough for Rockstar to issue an apology and announce improvements.[^46] A new version can increase availability while weakening the atmosphere people wanted to revisit.

Alongside the soundtrack alterations in IV, it raises a question the franchise's future should not evade: what does it mean to preserve a GTA? Its atmosphere depends on lighting, weather, voices, music, visual clutter and small incongruities. A cleaner texture does not necessarily preserve those relationships.

And there are the people making it all. The approach to VI's launch includes an employment dispute over Rockstar's dismissal of workers in the UK and Canada. The union alleges anti-union action; Rockstar disputes that and says the dismissals concerned confidentiality breaches. Reporting in October describes tribunal proceedings, not a final ruling resolving those competing accounts.[^47]

The contradiction is uncomfortable without requiring an invented verdict. GTA is expert at finding comedy in powerful organisations. The organisation producing GTA should receive the same scrutiny as any other employer.

## 11. What GTA VI has actually shown us

The return to Vice City is an attractive hook, but the public material describes a broader project: contemporary **Leonida**, with **Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos** at its centre. Rockstar's premise puts the pair inside a criminal conspiracy after an apparently straightforward score goes wrong.[^1]

The important change from 2002 is time. VI is returning to a place associated with nostalgia while setting its story in the contemporary world. The city can no longer rely on the eighties to supply a safely completed cultural identity.

### A criminal partnership, with somewhere to go wrong

Rockstar describes Jason as having grown up around criminals, served in the Army and drifted into drug-running work in the Keys. Lucia has recently left prison and wants a better life. Their relationship offers each a possible route forward.[^48]

That is an especially promising centre for GTA. The series is very good at writing unstable business relationships. A romantic partnership introduces a different set of questions: what do these people owe each other, what do they conceal, and what happens when a good decision for one threatens both?

The appeal is intimacy. A betrayal need not involve an entire criminal empire to matter. It may be enough that somebody you trust has lied about the money.

Lucia is also a major change in the authored leading cast. Calling her GTA's first playable woman would erase the optional women in the original PC game and the female characters players can create in Online. Her significance is as a central, extensively written female lead in the modern cinematic series.[^49]

That distinction gives the writers an opportunity to change who gets to have an inner life. It does not require Lucia to become a model citizen. A GTA protagonist can be selfish, cruel, funny and wrong. The improvement would be letting a woman possess the same range of dramatic possibilities that the men have enjoyed.

### Florida has more to offer than a compilation of arrests

Rockstar has introduced locations beyond Vice City, including **Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga**. Its published cast includes people involved in music, property, robbery, smuggling and conspiracy-minded online life.[^50]

The potential is geographic and social contrast. Wealth, tourism, industrial decline, wetlands, small communities and entertainment ambition can create different relationships to the same state. The danger is treating everything outside the glamorous centre as a supply of eccentric strangers to laugh at.

Rockstar's research account is encouraging here. Aaron Garbut has described trips through Miami and the Keys, aerial reference work and time spent with local people. Rupert Humphries has discussed choosing durable targets for satire rather than chasing short-lived memes.[^51]

Those are statements of intent. Their value will depend on whether Leonida feels observed at human scale. A crowd can contain a lot of polygons and very few people.

### We have moved beyond the first two trailers

The public record now includes **An Extended Look**, released on 27 August 2026, alongside press previews. Rockstar identifies the showcase as footage captured on PS5. Contemporary coverage describes a nearly twenty-seven-minute presentation combining narrative sequences, locations and stretches of play, including shootouts, pursuits and leisure activities.[^52]

That is substantial promotional evidence. It still cannot tell us how consistently the complete game will behave over dozens of hours. A demonstration can show an interaction working; it cannot establish how quickly the player will exhaust its variations.

More interesting than a tally of activities are Rockstar's comments about **mission flexibility**. Rob Nelson has described opening up routes where possible and trying to keep systems such as pursuits consistent between missions and free roam. The examples reported include multiple exits from a tower block and a getaway that can continue without following its suggested optimal route.[^53]

That addresses a longstanding fault line in the series. It suggests Rockstar knows the difference between showing the player something spectacular and letting the player make something happen. We will need the released game to judge how far it has gone.

Nelson has also said money will matter more to progression, including moments when players need to earn cash through activities such as stealing cars or robberies before continuing the story.[^54]

There is an excellent version of that idea. You need money; the city offers several risky opportunities; you choose one; the consequences become your story. Chinatown Wars already demonstrated how an economy can create reasons to move.

There is a tedious version, too. The next interesting mission is waiting, but first you must repeat a reliable cash-producing action until the number is large enough. A requirement to earn money becomes meaningful through the decisions it creates. Poverty alone is not a gameplay system, as several billion people could confirm.

### Stealing a car is becoming a decision again

The reported changes reach the activity on the box. Nelson has described tools and progression governing access to some parked vehicles, while carjacking a driver remains an option. A phone app can provide information about a vehicle's security and value.[^58]

That could make looking at a car more interesting than immediately acquiring it. Is it worth the risk, do you have what you need, and what happens if somebody notices? The possibility of making a poor choice is useful here. A theft game benefits from the occasional theft being a bad idea.

The wanted system is also being described in terms of information. According to Nelson's reported comments, crimes require a witness or alarm to reach the police. Preview coverage identifies recognition of clothing, faces, vehicles and the pair being together, alongside a return to a six-star maximum.[^59]

A pursuit can become more interesting if the police know something specific that you can do something about. Changing vehicles, breaking sight lines and moving through alleys could offer decisions beyond outrunning the nearest patrol car. Whether that information stays legible to the player will matter as much as its complexity.

For the driving itself, Nelson has pitched a combination of IV's sense of weight and V's accessibility.[^60] That is an appealing promise, and remains a developer's description rather than an independent verdict from someone holding the controller.

Refuelling has also appeared in preview reporting, with a brief fill-up sequence.[^61] Its value will depend less on the existence of a petrol station than on when the game makes you need one. Running low during an improvised escape could create a story. Interrupting an enjoyable drive for routine maintenance could simply interrupt an enjoyable drive. GTA has been negotiating this balance since Gary Penn complained about the original game's excessive simulation.

### The newest music news belongs in the picture

The musical plans are clearer than they were when the first trailer sent everyone back to Tom Petty. October's announcements include radio hosts and stations spanning Latin music, African music, metal, pop, Southern rap and country, with names including Bad Bunny, RaiNao, Burna Boy, Robyn, Trick Daddy and Trina.[^55]

On 15 October, Pitchfork reported Rockstar's reveal that **Justice composed the original score**, alongside news of the launch trailer and music from the game's album.[^56]

These are different layers of the soundtrack. The original score, songs made for an accompanying album, and the tracks available across in-game stations should not be collapsed into one number. An album track count is not the total number of songs in the game.

The artistic test is the old one. Does a station sound like part of a place? Can the radio suggest a community, an argument or a history? Celebrity names may attract the headline, but the pleasure of GTA radio often comes from a song you did not know, introduced by somebody you would cross the street to avoid.

### What the announcements cannot settle

The current official release page lists PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. A specific PC launch date should be treated as unconfirmed unless Rockstar announces it; October reporting about a possible PC window is reporting, not a platform announcement.[^1]

Likewise, the future multiplayer economy, the long-term place of roleplay, the full scope of interiors and the quality of individual systems should not be reconstructed from rumour lists. A visible building is not a promise that you can enter it. Two protagonists do not automatically mean cooperative story play.

We have enough real information to discuss VI intelligently. There is no need to promote every plausible wish into a feature.

## 12. What the next GTA should make possible

The history suggests more useful expectations than “everything, but bigger.”

### Let a failed plan become a good story

GTA's finest free-roam comedy comes from a system absorbing a mistake. A minor collision becomes an argument; an argument attracts police; escape requires abandoning the vehicle you meant to keep.

More of that resilience inside missions would be a genuine advance. A getaway should be able to become a different getaway. A surviving witness should sometimes create a complication rather than invalidate the scene. A player who solves a problem in an unexpected, believable way should not always be escorted back to the approved solution.

There will still be reasons to script an encounter tightly. A whole game needs rhythm, and performances need staging. The opportunity is to choose carefully where control matters and where a little disorder could improve the scene.

### Give wealth a scale we can feel

Money works when spending it reveals a priority. Do you replace a damaged car, improve a safe place, buy equipment for a riskier job or help somebody whose favour you might need?

The sums do not need to grow indefinitely. The early struggle for something modest can be more engaging than a late purchase that barely affects how you play. GTA has repeatedly made the climb vivid and abundance comparatively shapeless.

For Jason and Lucia, shared money could also expose character. Two people can agree that they need a better life while disagreeing completely about what one looks like.

Humphries has described the contemporary American dream in more modest terms: a home and freedom from debt, rather than extravagant success.[^57] There is considerable satirical potential in a world where the opening ambition of a criminal epic resembles the target on a mortgage calculator.

### Make the relationship worth the journey

A convincing partnership needs unremarkable moments. Familiar jokes. Small disagreements. The ease of sitting beside someone when neither has anything useful to say.

GTA already spends a lot of time in cars. That is an ideal space for intimacy. People can explain themselves badly, change the subject, argue about music and reveal what a grand confession would conceal.

The worst outcome would be a romance that exists only between gunfights, announced through dramatic declarations while the player does unrelated work. The best would let affection alter how ordinary play feels. A risky decision becomes different when somebody whose company you enjoy is in the passenger seat.

### Find sharper targets than the person nearest the camera

Contemporary satire faces a practical problem: reality can produce a more absurd story before a game has finished rendering its joke.

The answer is observation. There is plenty to say about how money buys innocence, how platforms reward humiliation, how precarious people sell success and how powerful people recast consequences as persecution. The mechanisms endure longer than a particular viral clip.

GTA also needs people who do more than embody the joke. IV's Roman remains funny because optimism and self-deception coexist with affection. Luis and Tony work because loyalty complicates exasperation. A whole city of loud idiots can be entertaining for an hour. Eventually somebody needs to make you care whether they get home.

The satire should also survive a glance at the checkout screen. A new Online can offer spectacular toys without making every evening a negotiation with an economy designed to consume it.

### Keep a future for the smaller story

The main release can be enormous. The franchise's future should have room for work that is more concentrated.

A Leonida story from another class position. A new business system attached to a small cast. An episode that examines one corner of the map properly. The Lost and Damned, Gay Tony and Chinatown Wars show that a GTA can distinguish itself through focus, viewpoint and design rather than a larger promotional number.

A city this expensive to create ought to be good for more than one kind of visit.

## The road back to Vice City

Across GTA's history, the same criminal fantasy has kept changing its address.

The original games offered the pleasure of disrupting a system. GTA III let us inhabit the disruption. Vice City made ownership irresistible. San Andreas gave ambition a home to answer to. IV asked what opportunity meant to somebody who had arrived too late and brought too much with him. V put the successful man beside the aspirant and the man who had abandoned most pretence. Online made the pursuit continue after the story should have ended.

VI's opportunity is to make all that scale answer to two people.

It can give us the spectacular state, the crowded beach, the wildlife, the music and the terrible decisions. The test is whether the details produce a place worth spending time in, and whether the people at its centre make leaving the next mission waiting feel like part of the experience.

For all its money and cultural weight, GTA still needs to understand the appeal of a stolen car with a good song playing.

We will probably crash it before the chorus. It would be nice to care who we crash it with.
