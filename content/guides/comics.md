# Character-first comics guide: the original X-Men, Justice League, and Avengers

**Edition:** 28 September 2026 · **Scope:** the teams' first published rosters and selected later landmarks. This is a *reading path*, not an inventory of every appearance or an assertion that decades-long superhero stories have definitive endings.

## How to read the paths

Read a team's **foundation** first. Then choose one member and follow the **Core** entries in order. **Optional** stories add depth, a famous alternative take, or a later status change; the reason is supplied. Issue numbers are the most reliable way to find a story even when a collected edition changes name or availability. A range is inclusive. A named trade can have different contents by printing, so check its contents page before buying. The paths use publication order broadly; flashback stories are identified as such.

**Three different meanings of “original”:**

| Team | First published lineup | Often confused with |
|---|---|---|
| X-Men, *X-Men* (1963) #1 | Cyclops, Marvel Girl/Jean Grey, Beast, Angel, Iceman; Professor X is their founder and mentor. | Wolverine, Storm, Colossus, Nightcrawler, and others arrive in the later “all-new, all-different” era. |
| Justice League of America, *The Brave and the Bold* (1955) #28 | Aquaman, Batman, Flash/Barry Allen, Green Lantern/Hal Jordan, Martian Manhunter, Superman, Wonder Woman. Batman and Superman have limited roles in the first story but belong to the founding seven. | *JLA: Year One* reflects a later continuity in which Black Canary replaces the Trinity among founders; New 52 *Justice League* #1–6 substitutes Cyborg for Martian Manhunter. |
| Avengers, *The Avengers* (1963) #1 | Ant-Man/Hank Pym, Wasp/Janet van Dyne, Iron Man/Tony Stark, Thor, Hulk/Bruce Banner. | Captain America joins in #4, after Hulk departs in #2. “Cap is a founder” is a common shorthand, not the original issue's roster. |

The lineup distinctions and DC's shifting origins are documented by [Marvel's original X-Men feature](https://www.marvel.com/articles/comics/original-x-men-1-first-look-at-all-new-multiversal-adventure-by-christos-gage-greg-land), [Marvel's Avengers origin list](https://www.marvel.com/comics/guides/1726/avengers-origin), and [DC's history of League formations](https://www.dc.com/blog/2021/03/17/us-united-how-almost-every-justice-league-was-formed).

## 1. Original X-Men

### Team spine

**Core:** *X-Men* (1963) #1 (school and Magneto) → #4–6 (Brotherhood and early team dynamics) → #12–13 (Juggernaut and Xavier's history) → #14–16 (Sentinels) → #56–66 (late original-run high point; skip for the short route) → *Giant-Size X-Men* #1 and *X-Men* #94 (the second team takes over) → *X-Factor* (1986) #1–6 (the five adults reunite) → *X-Factor* #18–26 (Apocalypse and Warren's transformation) → *X-Factor* #65–68 (“Endgame”) → *X-Men* (1991) #1–3 (the team reunifies). The 1963 series is reprint-only for #67–93, so do not hunt for new plot between #66 and #94.

**Optional:** *X-Men: First Class* (2006) and (2007) (warm modern stories about the school years, retrospective rather than the original publication sequence); *All-New X-Men* (2012) #1 onward → *X-Men: Blue* (2017) → *Extermination* #1–5 (the **time-displaced teenage originals**, a distinct later premise; not a substitute for the adult team's *X-Factor* history). [Marvel confirms](https://www.marvel.com/comics/issue/43462/slug) the time-travel setup and [the *Extermination* collection](https://www.marvel.com/comics/collection/70296/extermination_trade_paperback) closes that particular detour.

**Fastest worthwhile X-Men route:** *X-Men* #1, #12–16 → *Giant-Size X-Men* #1 → *Uncanny X-Men* #129–137 → *X-Factor* #1–6, #18–26, #65–68. This is a selective route with deliberate gaps; it follows the five from students to adult reunion without asking you to read sixty years of publication.

**Collecting starting point:** [*Mighty Marvel Masterworks: The X-Men Vol. 1*](https://www.marvel.com/comics/collection/93137/) collects #1–10; confirm the precise issue contents of subsequent volumes. For the adult reunion, look for collections titled *X-Factor Epic Collection* or check digital issue numbers. These are reading suggestions, not current price or stock claims.

## 2. Original Justice League of America

### Team spine and continuity choice

**Historical spine:** *The Brave and the Bold* #28–30 (debut) → *Justice League of America* (1960) #1 and #9 (first printed origin) → #21–22 (“Crisis on Earth-One!” / Earth-Two, the annual multiverse tradition) → #144 (a later origin revelation) → #200 (anniversary reunion). The [Silver Age omnibus vol. 1](https://www.dc.com/graphic-novels/justice-league-of-america-silver-age-omnibus-2014/justice-league-of-america-omnibus-vol-1) collects *Brave and the Bold* #28–30 and *JLA* #1–30.

**One modern team story, choose by taste:** *JLA* (1997) #1–4 and #5–9 (“New World Order” and early Morrison run: iconic seven-like lineup) → *JLA* #43–46 (“Tower of Babel”: Batman's contingency plans); **or** *DC: The New Frontier* #1–6 (beautiful, self-contained alternate historical retelling); **or** *Justice League* (2011) #1–6 (“Origin”: New 52 reboot with Cyborg, not Martian Manhunter). These are different continuity lenses, not sequential chapters of one unbroken origin. DC's [overview](https://www.dc.com/blog/2026-01-05/a-journey-of-justice-the-evolution-of-d-c-s-premiere-super-team) explains why the origin has changed.

The detective stories help explain the tension in *Tower of Babel*. |

## 3. Original Avengers

### Team spine

**Core:** *The Avengers* (1963) #1 (Loki assembles the five) → #2 (Hulk departs) → #3–4 (Hulk pursuit and Captain America's arrival) → #16 (the first dramatic roster turnover) → #54–58 (Ultron and Vision, the team's enduring family drama) → #137–151 (Beast's team years) → #273–277 (“Under Siege,” an Avengers Mansion crisis) → *Avengers* (1998) #1–4 (Heroes Return relaunch) → *Avengers* #500–503 and *Avengers Finale* #1 (“Disassembled,” a break before a new age).

**Optional:** *The Ultimates* (2002) #1–13 (influential **separate Ultimate universe**, not the 1963 team's continuity); *New Avengers* (2005) #1–6 (new roster after *Disassembled*); *Avengers* (2012) and *New Avengers* (2013) by Jonathan Hickman → *Secret Wars* (2015) #1–9 (ambitious, long multiverse climax; Tony, Thor, and the team matter, but this is not a tidy original-five reunion). [Marvel's origin guide](https://www.marvel.com/comics/guides/1726/avengers-origin) confirms the founding five; [its first omnibus](https://www.marvel.com/comics/collection/42234/) collects #1–30.

## Picking books without overbuying

- **One volume per team's origin:** *Mighty Marvel Masterworks: The X-Men Vol. 1* (#1–10), a collection containing *Brave and the Bold* #28–30 (for example the Silver Age JLA omnibus, which is much larger), and *Mighty Marvel Masterworks: The Avengers Vol. 1* (#1–10). A facsimile of *Brave and the Bold* #28 is a smaller entry point if you only want the debut.
- **For reading rather than collecting:** issue-based digital libraries or later reprints can be more practical than original Silver Age singles. Check the table of contents for the exact issues above before paying for an omnibus or trade.
- **For a father–son shelf later:** *The New Frontier*, *All-Star Superman*, and *X-Men: First Class* read well as distinct books; mature content and age suitability vary markedly in *Immortal Hulk*, *Uncanny X-Force*, *The Long Halloween*, and the Pym/Janet storyline.
