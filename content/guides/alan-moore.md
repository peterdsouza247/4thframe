**Start with *Watchmen* for a complete major work, or *Saga of the Swamp Thing Book One* for an ongoing character transformed by a new creative team.** Prefer something lighter on initial background? *Top 10* offers a self-contained first series about police work in a superhuman city.

This is a route through selected Moore comics, with major endings withheld. The works occupy separate worlds. There is no universal “Alan Moore order”, and no need to read his superhero work before his original graphic novels. Reviewed on **17 October 2026**.

For the critical discussion, read [The Best Alan Moore Comics](../../stories/best-alan-moore-comics/). For alternative writers, use [our five-author list](../../stories/comic-book-writers-worth-following/).

## Choose your first book

- **A formally precise superhero mystery:** *Watchmen* #1–12.
- **Horror, identity and unconventional romance:** *Saga of the Swamp Thing Book One*, then continue through Book Six if it catches you.
- **Political dystopia:** the complete *V for Vendetta*.
- **A long, adult historical fiction:** *From Hell*.
- **A busy, funny and sometimes melancholy procedural:** the original *Top 10* #1–12.
- **Visual experimentation and a magical worldview:** *Promethea* #1–32, after you decide that its changing focus suits you.

These are reading choices, not rungs of a ladder. *From Hell* demands time and tolerance for disturbing material; that does not make it more compulsory than a shorter work you actually want to read.

## Watchmen: one book is enough

Read the original twelve issues in order, including the prose documents placed between chapters. Those inserts contribute to the world's history and to the way characters present themselves. A complete standard paperback contains the core story. Deluxe and Absolute editions offer different presentation and supplementary material, not additional mandatory main chapters.[^1]

*Before Watchmen*, *Doomsday Clock*, *Rorschach* and screen adaptations are separate later projects. None is needed to complete Moore and Gibbons's original work. If you want more after finishing, choose them on their own merits rather than treating their existence as evidence of a missing original ending.

Avoid character biographies and explanatory videos until after the final chapter. Much of the pleasure depends on how the book controls information.

## Swamp Thing: follow one collection sequence

Read **Saga of the Swamp Thing Books One–Six** in numbered order. These are the familiar six-volume Moore collections. The opening Book One includes #20–27; “The Anatomy Lesson” is #21. Issue #20 closes the previous creative phase, so a little initial disorientation is normal.[^2]

You can also choose the **three Absolute Swamp Thing by Alan Moore volumes**. They overlap the six-book sequence and use a different visual presentation, including recolouring. Compare sample pages if the colour approach matters to you. “Absolute” here is a collected-edition format, unrelated to DC's newer Absolute Universe continuity.

For loose issues, Moore's main stretch occupies *Saga of the Swamp Thing/Swamp Thing* #20–64, with some issues by other writers and the connected Annual #2. The collections handle those boundaries and placement more easily than an indiscriminate issue-range purchase.

You do not need earlier Len Wein stories, *Crisis on Infinite Earths* in full, or a separate Hellblazer reading project before starting. The six-book sequence provides a clear endpoint for this author phase. Rick Veitch's continuation is the next creator's work, not a missing Moore book.

## V for Vendetta and From Hell

**V for Vendetta:** choose a complete collected edition. Older split editions exist, so confirm that the whole story is included. The David Lloyd artwork and original political argument differ from the film. You need no superhero reading. Expect fascist violence, abuse, sexual material and a deeply troubling coercive sequence.[^3]

**From Hell:** choose the original black-and-white presentation or **From Hell: Master Edition**, with colour and revisions by Eddie Campbell. Both present the central story; the Master Edition is not a sequel. Moore's annotations distinguish documented material from speculation and invention. They are valuable context, though you can read the narrative first and return to them.[^4]

Treat *From Hell* as historical fiction. Its theory of the Whitechapel murders is not established history. Graphic violence, sexual material and sustained misogyny are central to its subject. It is better approached in deliberate sessions than bought as an uncomplicated mystery thriller.

## Top 10 and Promethea

**Top 10:** begin with the original #1–12, available in a combined collection or the older first two volumes. *The Forty-Niners*, with Gene Ha, is a prequel that can be read afterwards; publication order lets its world feel familiar before you explore its earlier history. *Smax*, with Zander Cannon, is another Moore-written branch. Later continuations by other writers are separate selections.[^5]

**Promethea:** read #1–32 in order. Choose a collected sequence that explicitly covers the full range. Its opening superhero-like adventure develops into a substantial exploration of magic, symbolism and cosmology. If you want the opening tone to remain unchanged, the later emphasis may disappoint you. The visual page design is a major attraction, so inspect the reproduction size before choosing the smallest format.

Neither series is in continuity with *Watchmen* or *Swamp Thing*. Shared author and publisher labels do not create narrative dependencies.

## Miracleman, Halo Jones and shorter Superman stories

**Miracleman:** distinguish the Moore phase from **Miracleman by Gaiman and Buckingham**. Some Marvel editions credit Moore as **“The Original Writer”**. A collection that continues into Gaiman's work is not all written by Moore. Check the contents and credits rather than relying on a single name on a retailer's listing. Adult violence and sexual material require a considered recommendation; no later unfinished continuation should be mistaken for the boundary of Moore's own phase.

**The Ballad of Halo Jones:** the three existing books, by Moore and Ian Gibson, can be read in order. They constitute the published material rather than every part of the larger project originally contemplated. It is a good route into his science fiction if you prefer an ordinary protagonist to superhuman power.

**Short Superman route:** read “For the Man Who Has Everything” (*Superman Annual* #11) and “Whatever Happened to the Man of Tomorrow?” (*Superman* #423 and *Action Comics* #583). Collections under the second title often include both and other material. Dave Gibbons draws the annual; Curt Swan is the principal penciller of the two-part farewell. These are self-contained stories, though knowing the classic supporting cast adds feeling.[^6]

**The League of Extraordinary Gentlemen:** begin with Volume I, then Volume II. Kevin O'Neill's art is a major reason to read it. Later volumes grow increasingly dependent on literary allusions, and the series includes sexual violence and racist material embedded in and interrogating its sources. This is an adult optional branch, not the obvious next book for everyone who enjoyed *Watchmen*.

## A modest first shelf

Try **Watchmen → Swamp Thing Book One → Top 10**. The change in form and mood gives a better impression of Moore's range than buying three grim superhero books and calling the pattern established. Continue the project that interests you most.

No live stock or local prices are assumed here. Compare issue lists when buying used, especially where an edition's title lacks “complete”. A new cover, oversized format or expensive binding can improve presentation without adding a new story.
