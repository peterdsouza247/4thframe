**Best first book: *Fantastic Four by Jonathan Hickman: The Complete Collection Vol. 1*.** Prefer a shorter, more radical introduction? Read the original *House of X/Powers of X* collection. Want a finished original universe? Begin *East of West* at #1.

This guide offers routes through selected Hickman work, not a checklist of everything bearing his name. The books below occupy different continuities and can be read independently unless a dependency is explicitly stated. Major story revelations are withheld. Details reviewed on **17 October 2026**.

For the critical case behind these choices, read [The Best Jonathan Hickman Comics](../../stories/best-jonathan-hickman-comics/). Compare other writers in [our five-author starting list](../../stories/comic-book-writers-worth-following/).

## Choose a route

- **Family and ambitious science fiction:** *Fantastic Four/FF*, then decide whether to continue into the Avengers saga.
- **Mutant politics and big revelations:** the twelve-part *House of X/Powers of X*, then the selected X-Men route below.
- **The long Marvel construction:** *Fantastic Four/FF* → *Avengers/New Avengers*, including *Infinity* and *Time Runs Out* → *Secret Wars* (2015).
- **An independent, completed epic:** *East of West* #1–45.
- **A shorter original project:** *Decorum* #1–8, or *The Nightly News* for Hickman drawing as well as writing.

The long Marvel route is a recommendation for emotional development, not a claim that every title is mandatory homework. *Secret Warriors*, *S.H.I.E.L.D.* and the original Ultimate line add context or parallel ideas; you can understand the central Avengers-to-Secret-Wars story without completing them first.

## Fantastic Four and FF without losing half the story

The simplest collected route is **Fantastic Four by Jonathan Hickman: The Complete Collection, Volumes 1–4**, in numbered order. The alternative is **Fantastic Four by Jonathan Hickman Omnibus, Volumes 1–2**. Choose one sequence. Buying both duplicates the main material.

The opening includes *Dark Reign: Fantastic Four* #1–5. The main run moves through *Fantastic Four* #570–588, *FF* #1–23 and the resumed *Fantastic Four* #600–611, with #605.1 among the collected extras. The two series overlap after the original title returns. Follow the assembled books' ordering rather than reading all of one title and then all of the other.[^1]

The word **Complete** belongs to a four-volume collection sequence. Volume 1 alone is the opening portion. The older, shorter volumes titled simply *Fantastic Four by Jonathan Hickman* have different contents and numbering; an older “Vol. 4” is not the same as “Complete Collection Vol. 4”.

The end of this paired run is a genuine stopping point. Continue toward *Secret Wars* because you want a larger exploration of related characters and ideas, not because the family story has failed to finish.

## Avengers to Secret Wars: the manageable collected route

Read **Avengers by Jonathan Hickman: The Complete Collection Volumes 1–5**, followed by the standard **Secret Wars** collection containing the 2015 #1–9. An alternative is **Avengers by Jonathan Hickman Omnibus Volumes 1–2**, then those nine *Secret Wars* issues.

These routes assemble *Avengers* (2012), *New Avengers* (2013) and *Infinity*. Volume 3 of the Complete Collections contains *Infinity* #1–6 and its paired-series chapters; you do not need to purchase a second Infinity volume to satisfy the crossover. The closing *Time Runs Out* movement covers *Avengers* #35–44 and *New Avengers* #24–33.[^2]

**A potentially expensive overlap:** *Secret Wars by Jonathan Hickman Omnibus* includes *Time Runs Out* as well as the event. If you already own the full Avengers collections, it repeats that closing movement. The smaller nine-issue *Secret Wars* collection is the cleaner continuation.[^3]

The numerous Battleworld miniseries are optional expansions for this route. Choose them by character or premise after the main event if you want more. The 1984 *Secret Wars* and 2004 *Secret War* are different works, not preceding chapters of Hickman's nine-issue series.

## House of X/Powers of X: the exact twelve-part order

For a first reading, choose the original interleaved presentation. A later chronological rearrangement changes when information is revealed. Read loose issues in this order:[^4]

1. *House of X* #1.
2. *Powers of X* #1.
3. *House of X* #2.
4. *Powers of X* #2.
5. *Powers of X* #3.
6. *House of X* #3.
7. *House of X* #4.
8. *Powers of X* #4.
9. *House of X* #5.
10. *Powers of X* #5.
11. *House of X* #6.
12. *Powers of X* #6.

No earlier X-Men run is required. A broad understanding that Xavier, Magneto and the X-Men disagree about mutant survival is enough. Let the books introduce their new circumstances before consulting character histories; those histories can spoil the central discoveries.

## A selected Hickman X-Men continuation

After HoX/PoX, read *X-Men* (2019) #1–11. The *X of Swords* crossover follows, with *X-Men* #12–15 embedded in the full event. Then return to *X-Men* #16–21. Read the **2021 Hellfire Gala** material, including *Planet-Size X-Men* #1, for the shared status quo before **Inferno** (2021) #1–4.

That is a practical spine, not every Hickman-written mutant issue. His *Giant-Size X-Men* specials and selected *New Mutants* chapters are optional expansions. *X of Swords* and the Gala involve other writers. A collection labelled *X-Men by Jonathan Hickman* should not be assumed to contain every chapter of those line-wide events.

*Inferno* is the end of Hickman's principal Krakoan phase, not the finale of Krakoa itself. The later *Fall of the House of X* and *Rise of the Powers of X* are by other writers. Do not substitute them for the 2019 opening because the titles look similar.[^5]

## Independent books and other Marvel branches

**East of West:** read #1–45 in order, using ten standard trades, three *Apocalypse: Year* hardcovers, or the *End Times Compendium*. These are alternative formats for the same main story. The setting has no Marvel dependency. Expect graphic violence and a dense opening.[^6]

**Secret Warriors:** begin the 2009 series, not a later relaunch with the same title. The two Complete Collections gather the main #1–28 run and related material. It is a finished espionage project. Knowing Fury is a veteran spymaster helps; reading all of *Secret Invasion* first is unnecessary for this author route.

**S.H.I.E.L.D.:** *Architects of Forever* → *The Human Machine*. This is Hickman and Dustin Weaver's historical-cosmic story, with two separately numbered series. Follow the collections rather than assuming that two #1 issues are duplicates.[^7]

**Decorum:** #1–8, collected together. Finite science fiction with Mike Huddleston; no shared-universe background.

**Ultimate Spider-Man (2024):** begin at #1 or *Vol. 1: Married with Children*. *Ultimate Invasion* #1–4 and *Ultimate Universe* (2023) #1 explain the wider setting and are useful background, but the Spider-Man opening introduces Peter's situation. This is separate from Bendis's 2000 series. Marvel bills #24 as the conclusion of the Hickman/Checchetto run; the wider line has additional event material beyond this author selection.[^8]

**The Black Monday Murders:** the original two trades collect #1–8. Treat them as an unfinished story. Coker discussed returning to conclude the project in August 2026, but an intention to finish is not a finished book. Verify any newly announced final collection's contents before buying specifically for closure.[^9]

## Before ordering

Choose a route and one collection format. Check the printed issue list, especially for *Infinity*, *Time Runs Out* and the several types of Fantastic Four collection. No local price or current Indian stock is assumed here. A scarce omnibus is not the only way to read the work; standard trades and legitimate digital editions can contain the same story.

Many independent titles are adult reading. Do not infer suitability for children from a shared superhero byline. If you only want one complete purchase, *Decorum* offers the simplest finite science-fiction choice, while HoX/PoX offers a complete opening movement with an intentionally continuing world.
