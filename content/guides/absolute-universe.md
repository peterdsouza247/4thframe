**Pick one hero and start at issue #1 or Volume 1.** That is the useful answer to the Absolute Universe reading-order question. The universe has a creation story, growing connections and additional specials, but its opening books were built to introduce their own characters. Start with the one whose premise makes you want to turn a page.

For a first taste, choose *Absolute Batman* for Gotham action and grotesque threats, *Absolute Wonder Woman* for mythic fantasy, or *Absolute Martian Manhunter* for an unsettling experiment with perception. Those are recommendations for different reading appetites, rather than a requirement to collect three series at once.

This guide covers the six original titles, the 2026 Green Arrow and Catwoman expansions, their most useful companion reading, and the announced *Absolute Justice* event. Publication details were reviewed **17 October 2026**. Opening premises are discussed; later identity twists and story outcomes are left alone.

## What Absolute changes

The Absolute Universe gives familiar DC heroes different circumstances and lets their stories develop in a separate continuity. Its cosmic origin is tied to Darkseid and *DC All In Special* #1, but the practical appeal is easier to grasp: the heroes must emerge in a world built to make their work harder.

Bruce Wayne lacks his usual fortune. Diana grows up far from the Amazon society that normally shapes her. Superman encounters an Earth whose exploitation echoes the world he lost. Changing these foundations allows the creators to ask which parts of the heroes survive when their familiar support systems disappear.

The books also have different genres and visual identities. Absolute does not prescribe one colour palette or one degree of misery. A vast sword, a hostile alien presence and a young man running from his past can belong to the same publishing line without becoming the same comic.

**Absolute Universe** is also different from DC's **Absolute Edition** format. An oversized *Absolute Batman: The Long Halloween* collection would still contain the familiar *Long Halloween* story. *Absolute Batman* by Scott Snyder and Nick Dragotta is the alternate-universe series covered here. Check the creators and issue contents when shopping.

## Choose your first book

[BOOKS]

### Absolute Batman

Scott Snyder and Nick Dragotta rebuild Bruce around a working-class life and a different relationship with Gotham. His methods and equipment grow from those circumstances. The exaggerated physical design is part of the proposition: this Batman occupies space like an argument that has become too large for the room.

**Choose it for:** a recognisable central hero, aggressive action and a Gotham that leans into horror. **Begin with:** *The Zoo*, collecting #1–6. Continue with the series in numerical order. The absence of Bruce's usual wealth changes the story's resources and relationships; it does not mean the book abandons spectacular superhero invention.

### Absolute Wonder Woman

Kelly Thompson and Hayden Sherman introduce Diana through exile, magic and an upbringing in Hell. Her unusual weapons and silhouette are easy to notice; the more interesting entry point is what this upbringing does to her understanding of care, duty and the surface world.

**Choose it for:** fantasy, monsters and a heroine whose background has been substantially rebuilt. **Begin with:** *The Last Amazon*, collecting #1–7. Its issue count differs from the six-issue first volumes of the other original titles, so do not assume each Volume 1 ends at the same number.

### Absolute Superman

Jason Aaron and Rafa Sandoval make Kal-El's Kryptonian past and his encounters with exploitation on Earth central to the new premise. The Lazarus Corporation supplies a form of power that can operate through institutions, workers and armed enforcers, giving Superman something larger than one opponent to confront.

**Choose it for:** science fiction, displacement and the social stakes of immense power. **Begin with:** *Last Dust of Krypton*, collecting #1–6. A passing knowledge of Superman is sufficient; the differences in this version's history are part of what the book explains.

### Absolute Flash

Jeff Lemire and Nick Robles put Wally West at the centre of a teenage story without his familiar place in an established Flash family. Starting here means meeting Wally as this book defines him, rather than trying to match each development to decades of Speed Force history.

**Choose it for:** a younger protagonist, uncertainty and a story built around movement and pursuit. **Begin with:** *Of Two Worlds*, collecting #1–6. The Flash's mainstream continuity can wait outside with the other reading lists.

### Absolute Green Lantern

Al Ewing and Jahnoy Lindsay bring an alien intrusion and a strong sense of dread to the Green Lantern premise. Jo Mullein is central to this version of the mythos. Treat the familiar names as clues to a new arrangement, rather than a guarantee that the Corps will work as you remember.

**Choose it for:** a disturbing encounter with cosmic power and a mystery that develops through its own rules. **Begin with:** *Without Fear*, collecting #1–6. You do not need to read *Far Sector* or a previous Green Lantern run first.

### Absolute Martian Manhunter

Deniz Camp and Javier Rodríguez link FBI agent John Jones to an alien consciousness whose perception makes ordinary reality unstable. The images are part of the thinking: the premise needs a page capable of showing something a conventional explanation cannot comfortably contain.

**Choose it for:** strange science fiction, visual experimentation and an alien presence that feels alien. **Begin with:** *Martian Vision*, collecting #1–6, followed by *The Agency*, collecting #7–12. This twelve-issue run has concluded, making it the clearest original-line choice for readers who want a complete run now.

## A manageable reading order

### Follow one hero

Read that series from #1 in numerical order, or its collected volumes in order. The first arcs introduce their own casts. You can finish an opening volume before sampling another book. This gives each title's atmosphere room to establish itself.

### Sample the original six

Try the first volumes of *Batman*, *Wonder Woman* and *Superman*, then *Flash*, *Martian Manhunter* and *Green Lantern*. This roughly follows the two launch waves. It is a suggested sampling order, not an in-universe chronology, and you can swap titles without losing an opening chapter.

### Follow the wider universe

Read *DC All In Special* #1 for the cosmic framing, then the original titles' opening arcs. Add *Absolute Evil* after you are familiar with the world, before starting *Absolute Green Arrow*. Follow the specific crossover and companion placements below. There is little benefit in interrupting every six-issue arc to alternate books mechanically.

## Crossovers, annuals and companion stories

### DC All In Special #1

Optional context for a reader following a single Absolute hero; useful if the universe's relationship to Darkseid and the main DC line interests you. You do not need an *Absolute Power* shopping list before opening *Absolute Batman* #1. *Absolute Power* is a separate event despite the shared word in its title.

### Absolute Evil #1

Al Ewing and Giuseppe Camuncoli's one-shot broadens the view toward the forces opposing the heroes. Read it after sampling the original books. It becomes particularly relevant when moving into *Absolute Green Arrow*, which DC explicitly presents as spinning out of its events. The special contains revelations, so resist its online summaries until you are ready.

### Wonder Woman meets Batman

Read **Absolute Wonder Woman #15, then Absolute Batman #16**. DC identifies that Batman issue as the second chapter. This is a real cross-title dependency, so a reader following only one series should make the short detour. Reach the relevant point in your chosen hero's run first; do not jump from Volume 1 straight to the meeting.

### Batman's annual and Ark M special

*Absolute Batman 2025 Annual* #1 and *Absolute Batman: Ark M Special* #1 expand the Batman corner. They are companion reading after you have met this Gotham, rather than alternative starting points. Follow the contents of your later Batman collections and check for these stories before purchasing separate copies. An annual is a new story package; a second printing or variant cover is another edition of an existing issue.

### Free Comic Book Day editions

Sampler specials can combine material from different parts of DC's publishing line. Inspect the contents and any reprint notice. They are useful extras, but this guide's opening routes do not require tracking down a promotional edition before starting a regular series.

## The 2026 expansion

### Absolute Catwoman

Che Grayson, Scott Snyder and Bengal give Selina Kyle her own six-issue miniseries. Her premise emphasises exceptional resources and skills, with a past that threatens what she has built. For readers already following Gotham, DC places this story after Selina's appearance in **Absolute Batman #14**. That is the useful lead-in, rather than a requirement to read every Absolute title.

Read **Catwoman #1–3**, then **Absolute Cassandra Cain: The Shadow's Hand #1**, then **Catwoman #4–6 as available**. DC announced the Cassandra Cain one-shot specifically between issues #3 and #4. The final issues of the Catwoman miniseries are not all released at this guide's review date. This is its intended route, not a claim that the complete six-issue story is already on shelves.

### Absolute Green Arrow

Pornsak Pichetshote and Rafael Albuquerque take the premise into an urban horror murder mystery, with Dinah Lance investigating a killer targeting corrupt billionaires. Start with **Absolute Evil #1**, then **Absolute Green Arrow #1 onward**. DC introduced the launch as a limited series; check current solicitations for its eventual collected contents rather than assuming its whole run is already collected.

These additions are good second purchases when their genres appeal. The original six first volumes remain the simplest way to choose an initial Absolute book.

## What comes next: Absolute Justice

At New York Comic Con 2026, DC announced *Absolute Justice*, a five-issue event by Jason Aaron and Juan Ferreyra for **April 2027**. The Comics Beat's on-site report says the series will bring the heroes together and is planned without crossover tie-ins interrupting their ongoing titles.

It is **announced reading**, not a released chapter to insert into today's route. Earlier publicity teased an event in late 2026; the newer named announcement is the relevant information for this guide. Final scheduling and collected contents can change before publication.

## The easiest way to collect it

Buy one opening collection, finish it, and choose a second title by genre. Paperback and hardcover versions often contain the same story. Variant covers and later printings do not add chapters. The most expensive copy of #1 is not a more complete introduction to its universe.

Check each book's issue list, especially around the Wonder Woman/Batman meeting and the Gotham specials. A collection may include a companion story that a single-issue checklist lists separately. You are buying reading material, not trying to placate a multiversal filing cabinet.
