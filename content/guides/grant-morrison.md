**Start with *All-Star Superman*.** It is twelve issues, complete in one collection, and needs no earlier continuity. For a longer route through Morrison's ideas about fiction, follow it with *Animal Man*. For a very short, emotionally severe story, choose *We3*.

The books below form an author route, not a shared chronology. Morrison's Superman, Vertigo-era projects and Marvel work can be read independently. This guide withholds major endings and avoids a list of every crossover appearance. Reviewed on **17 October 2026**.

Read [The Best Grant Morrison Comics](../../stories/best-grant-morrison-comics/) for the critical discussion, or compare writers in [our starting list](../../stories/comic-book-writers-worth-following/).

## Pick your level of commitment

- **One welcoming complete book:** *All-Star Superman* #1–12.
- **A short original story:** *We3* #1–3. Graphic violence and animal suffering make this an emotionally difficult entry point.
- **A medium-length conceptual journey:** *Animal Man* #1–26 and its collected *Secret Origins* story.
- **The outsider team:** *Doom Patrol* #19–63 and *Doom Force Special* #1.
- **Marvel's mutant culture:** *New X-Men* #114–154 and Annual 2001.
- **A long superhero construction:** the Batman route below.
- **Advanced, adult counterculture:** *The Invisibles*, after you have decided that its subject and density appeal.

## The independent starting shelf

**All-Star Superman:** read #1–12 in order, or the complete collection. The earlier *All Star Superman Vol. 1* contains only the opening portion. Look for a listing that explicitly includes all twelve issues. Morrison's *Action Comics* run is a separate project, not the next chapter.[^1]

**We3:** read all three issues together. It has no Superman or DC universe prerequisite. Its compact length makes it accessible in continuity terms, but it is a poor surprise gift for someone sensitive to violence against animals.

**Animal Man:** Morrison begins at #1 of the 1988 series and finishes at #26. The omnibus also contains material from *Secret Origins* #39. Follow the assembled volume's placement of that story. The later Jeff Lemire series is a separate run. Morrison's endpoint is a suitable stopping place even though the character continues.[^2]

**Doom Patrol:** begin at #19 of the 1987 series. You do not need the first eighteen issues to begin Morrison's phase. The omnibus collects #19–63 plus *Doom Force Special* #1. *Crawling from the Wreckage* is the familiar opening trade if you prefer a smaller sample. Rachel Pollack's subsequent run is worth pursuing on its own terms, but it is not another Morrison volume.[^3]

## New X-Men: start at 114, not 1

Morrison's Marvel run begins when *X-Men* changes its title to **New X-Men at #114**. Read #114–154 and Annual 2001 using an author collection's placement. The omnibus identifies that exact scope. The annual is additional story material, not a duplicate of an ordinary numbered issue.[^4]

*E Is for Extinction* supplies the opening arc. The subsequent collected path includes *Imperial*, *New Worlds*, *Riot at Xavier's*, *Assault on Weapon Plus*, *Planet X* and *Here Comes Tomorrow*, but printings group these differently. Use issue contents rather than assuming that any product labelled “Book Two” matches a different publisher format's “Volume Two”.

The 2004 series *New X-Men: Academy X* and other books sharing the *New X-Men* words are different projects. You can stop at #154 without following every later change to the revelations in this run. Some later character summaries spoil it, so avoid them while reading.

This is a useful companion to Hickman's HoX/PoX, but neither is a strict prerequisite for the other. Morrison's focus on mutant culture and Hickman's mutant nationhood make a thematic pairing rather than a single author sequence.

## Batman: use the assembled route

For the full project, the simplest purchase plan is **Batman by Grant Morrison Omnibus Volumes 1–3**, with the main **Final Crisis** collection as a separate contextual bridge. The omnibuses gather Morrison's Batman chapters; they do not contain every DC event chapter surrounding them.

1. **Omnibus Vol. 1:** the opening phase, including *Batman and Son*, the Black Glove material and *Batman R.I.P.*. It collects *Batman* #655–658 and #663–683 plus selected related material. Some end chapters overlap the Final Crisis period; accept them as the collection's presentation rather than inventing a perfectly linear timeline.
2. **Final Crisis:** read the assembled main event here if you want the larger transition behind the next Batman phase. Choose a collection including *Superman Beyond* #1–2 and *Final Crisis: Submit* alongside the main #1–7. This event is deliberately dense. Readers focused solely on Batman may use the collection's recap and proceed, but should understand that they are skipping context.
3. **Omnibus Vol. 2:** *Batman and Robin* #1–16, *The Return of Bruce Wayne* #1–6 and *Batman* #700–702. Follow the omnibus's interleaving. Reading all of *Return* and then all of *Batman and Robin* changes the intended middle movement.
4. **Omnibus Vol. 3:** *Batman Incorporated*, from the original series through the New 52 continuation. This is the completion of Morrison's central Batman project.[^5]

For a **short sample**, *Batman and Robin: Batman Reborn* works with a minimal premise: Dick Grayson is Batman and Damian Wayne is Robin. It is the beginning of this partnership, not the beginning of Morrison's whole run. Enjoying it is a good reason to return to the earlier material.

For older individual trades, the broad progression is *Batman and Son* / *The Black Glove* → *Batman R.I.P.* → the Final Crisis transition → *Batman and Robin* interleaved with *The Return of Bruce Wayne* → *Batman Incorporated*. Editions differ, so an omnibus is easier than assembling this from similarly titled used paperbacks.

## Seven Soldiers and The Invisibles

**Seven Soldiers:** use the omnibus or an assembled collection's sequence. The project comprises *Seven Soldiers* #0, seven four-issue miniseries, and *Seven Soldiers* #1: thirty issues in total. The seven branches are *Shining Knight*, *Guardian*, *Zatanna*, *Klarion*, *Mister Miracle*, *Bulleteer* and *Frankenstein*. The concluding #1 is the ending, despite its number. For a first reading, preserve the collection's order rather than finishing each miniseries separately.[^6]

**The Invisibles:** begin with Volume 1 of the original series, then Volume 2, then Volume 3. The third series counts down from #12 to #1. That countdown is intentional. Modern collected editions may use a different volume system, so follow the numbered books within one format. Expect sexual material, torture and violence; this is an adult, substantial commitment.

Neither project requires you to solve every allusion before proceeding. Read the scene first. Revisit an explanation when a recurring image or idea has become interesting, rather than halting every page to search for a definitive answer.

## What to leave until later

*Final Crisis* and *The Multiversity* are stronger recommendations after you have developed an appetite for Morrison's treatment of superhero history. *JLA* offers a more direct route into large-scale team adventures if the experimental books are not your priority. *Arkham Asylum*, with Dave McKean, is its own disturbing, expressionist Batman work, not a missing opening chapter of the later run.

The reading order between separate projects is yours to choose. A practical first shelf is *All-Star Superman* → *Animal Man* → *Doom Patrol* or *New X-Men*. That sequence increases commitment without pretending that comic-reading is an examination with prescribed prerequisites.
